Some emotions use avoir (to have) instead of être. Think of it as "having" a feeling rather than "being" one:

⚠️ Être vs. Avoir — Don't Mix Them!

Je suis excité(e) exists but can have sexual connotations in French! For "I'm excited (about something)," prefer: Je suis enthousiaste, J'ai hâte, or Ça me fait plaisir. This catches many English speakers off guard.

🇫🇷 France — Emotional Expression

The French tend toward understatement: "Ça va" (it's going) covers everything from "fine" to "surviving." "Je suis un peu déçu" (I'm a bit disappointed) can mean deeply unhappy. "Pas mal" (not bad) is often genuine praise. Common intensifiers: vraiment (really), tellement (so much), carrément (totally, slang).

🇨🇦 Canada — Emotional Expression

Québécois French is often more expressive and direct. "J'suis ben content !" (I'm really happy — ben = bien). "Ça m'écoeure !" can mean "that disgusts me" or "that's amazing!" depending on tone. "C'est l'fun !" (it's fun) is ubiquitous. The sacres (swear words from religious terms) are used as intensifiers: "C'est crissement bon !"

💡 "Copain / Copine" — Context Matters!

Mon copain can mean "my friend" or "my boyfriend" depending on context. Among adults, it usually implies a romantic partner. For clarity: un ami = friend, mon copain = usually boyfriend. Adding petit(e) always means romantic: mon petit ami = my boyfriend.

🇫🇷 France — PACS

The PACS (Pacte Civil de Solidarité) is a civil union available to all couples in France since 1999. Hugely popular — nearly as many couples get PACSed as married. It offers many legal benefits of marriage with an easier dissolution process. "On est pacsés" is a common relationship status.

🇨🇦 Canada — Conjoint(e) de fait

In Québec, conjoint(e) de fait (common-law partner) is very common. Québec has the lowest marriage rate in Canada. Many couples live together long-term without marrying. "Mon chum" (boyfriend) and "ma blonde" (girlfriend) are the classic Québécois terms — yes, blonde means girlfriend regardless of hair color!

⚠️ "Tu Me Manques" — Reversed Logic!

"I miss you" in French is Tu me manques — literally "You are missing to me." The subject is the person being missed, not the one doing the missing. Mes parents me manquent = I miss my parents (my parents are missing to me). This trips up English speakers constantly!

🇫🇷 France — Friendship Culture

French friendships tend to form slowly but run deep. The apéro (pre-dinner drinks) is the social ritual — "On prend un apéro ?" is the classic invite. La bise (cheek kisses) between friends is standard greeting practice. Close male friends may use mon pote (my buddy).

🇨🇦 Canada — Friendship Culture

Québécois culture tends to be warmer and more open initially — closer to North American norms. "Mon chum" can mean a male friend (not just boyfriend) depending on context. "On va prendre une bière ?" (Want to grab a beer?) is the go-to social invite. Le 5 à 7 (5 to 7 PM happy hour) is a popular post-work gathering tradition.

🗣️ Expressing Emotions in Conversation

— Comment tu vas ? Tu as l'air triste.
(How are you? You look sad.)

— Oui, je suis un peu déprimé. Ma copine et moi, on s'est séparés.
(Yeah, I'm a bit down. My girlfriend and I broke up.)

— Oh non, je suis désolé. Ça fait combien de temps ?
(Oh no, I'm sorry. How long ago?)

— Deux semaines. C'est dur, mais bon, il faut tourner la page.
(Two weeks. It's hard, but well, I have to move on.)

— Devine quoi ! Marc m'a demandé en mariage !
(Guess what! Marc proposed to me!)

— Ah, c'est génial ! Félicitations ! Ça a été un coup de foudre, vous deux ?
(That's amazing! Congratulations! Was it love at first sight, you two?)

— Pas du tout, on était amis pendant trois ans avant de sortir ensemble !
(Not at all, we were friends for three years before dating!)

✍️ Practice: How Do You Feel?

Exercise 1 — être or avoir?

  1. I'm happy: je _____ content(e). (Answer: suis)
  2. I'm afraid: j'_____ peur. (Answer: ai)
  3. She's tired: elle _____ fatiguée. (Answer: est)
  4. We're in love: nous _____ amoureux. (Answer: sommes)
  5. I miss you: tu me _____. (the French says it backwards) (Answer: manques)
  6. In Québec, a boyfriend is un _____. (Answer: chum)
