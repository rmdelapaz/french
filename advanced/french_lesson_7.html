🇫🇷 Lesson 7: Les Accords Difficiles

French agreement has a long tail. You settled the main body of it long ago — adjectives, past participles with être, the preceding-COD rule with avoir. What is left is a set of roughly a dozen words that agree sometimes, according to what job they are doing in the sentence: tout, même, quelque, tel, demi, nu, ci-joint, the numerals, the colours.

This is the material of the French dictée, of every concours, and of the moment a French colleague glances at your email and decides whether you write well. It is finite. Learn it once.

1️⃣ Tout — Three Words in One Spelling

JobAgrees?Example
Determiner (all, the whole)yestoute la journée · tous les jours · toutes mes amies
Pronoun (everything, everyone)yesTous sont venus. · Toutes le savent.
Adverb (= quite, entirely)normally noIls sont tout étonnés. · Elle parle tout bas.

⚠️ The one exception that everyone gets wrong

Adverbial tout is invariable — except before a feminine adjective beginning with a consonant or an h aspiré, where it agrees for purely euphonic reasons:

Elle est tout étonnée. (vowel → invariable)
Elle est toute surprise. (consonant → agrees)
Elles sont toutes honteuses. (h aspiré → agrees)

And two fixed traps: tout autre chose = quite another thing (adverb), but toute autre solution = any other solution (determiner). Tous les deux, tout le monde, en tout cas are frozen.

3️⃣ Quelque and Quel que

5️⃣ Ci-joint, Ci-inclus

Before the noun it is treated as an adverb and stays invariable; after the noun it is an adjective and agrees.

Ci-joint la facture. — enclosed, the invoice.
Vous trouverez la facture ci-jointe. — you will find the enclosed invoice.
Ci-joint les documents demandés. — invariable, because it opens the sentence.

8️⃣ Participles Before an Infinitive

The subtlest rule in the set, and the one that appears most often in exams.

ParticipleRuleExample
fait + infinitivealways invariableJe les ai fait venir.
laissé + infinitiveagrees if the object does the action (1990: may stay invariable)Je les ai laissés partir.
vu, entendu + infinitiveagrees if the object does the actionJe les ai vus partir. / Les airs que j’ai entendu jouer.
dû, pu, vouluinvariable when the infinitive is understoodJ’ai fait tous les efforts que j’ai pu.

🇫🇷 France

These rules carry real social weight in France. Agreement errors in a professional email are noticed, and the Certificat Voltaire — a commercial spelling exam — is now listed on French CVs precisely because employers treat orthography as a proxy for rigour. The 1990 reform made laissé + infinitive optionally invariable, but the older agreeing form remains commoner in careful writing.

🇨🇦 Canada

Québec applies the same rules and teaches them at least as rigorously; provincial ministry exams test them explicitly. Québec was, however, quicker than France to adopt the 1990 rectifications in school materials and in government style guides, so a Québécois text is somewhat more likely to write maitre and cout without the circumflex. Both spellings are correct on both sides of the Atlantic.

✍️ Practice

Exercise 1 — tout

Write tout, toute, tous or toutes.

  1. Elle a travaillé _____ la journée. (Answer: toute)
  2. Elles sont _____ étonnées. (Answer: tout)
  3. Elles sont _____ surprises. (Answer: toutes)
  4. _____ les étudiants sont là. (Answer: tous)
  5. C’est _____ autre chose. (Answer: tout)

Exercise 2 — demi, nu, ci-joint, numerals

  • Il a attendu une _____-heure. (Answer: demi)
  • Il est deux heures et _____. (Answer: demie)
  • Les enfants courent _____-pieds. (Answer: nu)
  • Vous trouverez la facture ci-_____. (Answer: jointe)
  • Le livre fait deux _____ pages. (Answer: cents)
  • Il a quatre-vingt-_____ ans. (Answer: deux)

Exercise 3 — Colours and participles

  1. Elle portait des gants orange — agreement? Write invariable or accordé. _____ (Answer: invariable)
  2. des chemises bleu clair → _____ (Answer: invariable)
  3. des écharpes roses → _____ (Answer: accordé|accorde)
  4. Je les ai _____ venir. (faire) (Answer: fait)
  5. Je les ai _____ partir. (voir) (Answer: vus)

Exercise 4 — Homophones

  • _____ à moi, je refuse. (Answer: quant)
  • Je préfère partir _____ que d’attendre. (Answer: plutôt|plutot)
  • Il est arrivé _____ tôt que prévu. (Answer: plus)
  • Elle est _____ à signer. (Answer: prête|prete)
  • Je _____ ai déjà écrit deux fois. (to them) (Answer: leur)
