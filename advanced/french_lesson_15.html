🇫🇷 Lesson 15: Orthographe, Ponctuation & Typographie

At C2, what betrays a non-native written French is rarely grammar. It is the surface: a missing space before a colon, English quotation marks, a capital letter on Français where it should be lower case, a semicolon used as a comma. None of it affects comprehension. All of it is noticed instantly by a French reader, because French typography is codified and taught.

This lesson is the code, plus the spelling reform you need a position on, plus a proofreading method.

📏 The 1990 Rectifications

In 1990 the Conseil supérieur de la langue française published a set of rectifications orthographiques, endorsed by the Académie. They are recommended, not compulsory: both spellings are correct, and you will meet both. Schools in France and Québec now teach the new forms.

💡 Your practical position

Pick one system and be internally consistent inside a document. Nobody will object to either; everybody will notice if maitre and maître appear on the same page. Note the survivors: dû, sûr, mûr and jeûne keep the circumflex because it distinguishes them from du, sur, mur and jeune.

␣ The Space Rule — the Single Biggest Tell

French puts a thin non-breaking space before certain punctuation marks. English does not. This one rule, applied, makes your French look professionally set.

Note also: French uses a space or a thin space as the thousands separator and a comma as the decimal point — 1 250,75 € where English writes €1,250.75.

❝ Guillemets and Quotation

French quotes with « », not with " ". Inside a quotation, a second level uses English double quotes or single ones. And dialogue in literature uses the tiret cadratin rather than quotes at all.

Il m’a répondu : « Je n’en sais rien. »

Dialogue convention:

— Tu viens ?
— Non, je reste.

An interpolated incise inside a quotation takes commas and no extra quotes: « Je viendrai, dit-il, mais plus tard. »

Accents on capitals: keep them. À bientôt, ÉTAT, Île-de-France. The idea that capitals go unaccented is a typewriter-era limitation, and the Académie is explicit that the accent is part of the spelling.

⏸️ Punctuation as Logic

MarkWhat it does in French
le point-virgulejoins two balanced, independent clauses that belong together. Used far more than in English and a marker of good style.
les deux pointsannounces: an explanation, a list, a quotation, a consequence. French uses it where English would write because or namely.
le tiret cadratininterrupts, or introduces dialogue. In pairs, it brackets an aside more strongly than commas.
les points de suspensiontrails off or suggests. Three dots, never four, and no space before.
la virgulenever before et in a simple list: du pain, du vin et du fromage.

⚠️ The colon habit is worth stealing

Le projet a échoué : personne ne l’avait financé. An English writer would use because. The French colon states the causal relation without naming it, and it is one of the cheapest ways to make your prose read as French rather than as translated English.

✅ The Three-Pass Proofread

Do not proofread once for everything; you will catch nothing.

  1. Pass 1 — sense. Read it as a reader. Is the argument in order? Are the connectives honest (lesson 4)?
  2. Pass 2 — agreements. Read backwards, sentence by sentence, looking only at endings: participles, adjectives, tout, leur, numerals. Reading backwards stops your brain from reading meaning instead of letters.
  3. Pass 3 — typography. Search the document for ; : ! ? and check the spaces; check the quotation marks are « »; check capitals and accents.

🌟 If you want to go further

The Lexique des règles typographiques en usage à l’Imprimerie nationale is the reference professional French typesetters actually use, and Québec's Banque de dépannage linguistique puts the same rules online for free. Between them they settle every question on this page — including the ones nobody thinks to ask, like how to abbreviate numéro (no, never No.).

🇫🇷 France

Spelling carries unusual social weight in France, where the dictée is a national ritual and the Certificat Voltaire appears on CVs. Adoption of the 1990 reform has been slow and patchy in the press — Le Monde largely keeps traditional spellings — while school textbooks have moved. The Imprimerie nationale's rules are the professional standard for typography.

🇨🇦 Canada

Québec adopted the 1990 rectifications faster and more thoroughly, so Québécois publications are noticeably more likely to write cout and maitre. Québec typography also diverges slightly under North American pressure: the thin space before ; and ! is sometimes dropped, and the dollar sign follows the number (25 $) where Europe puts the euro after too (25 €) but English Canada puts the dollar first. The OQLF's guidance is the reference.
