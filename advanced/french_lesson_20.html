🇫🇷 Lesson 20: Lire la Littérature : la Prose

You can already read a French novel. B2 lesson 18 handed you the passé simple and with it the door to authentic literature. What this lesson adds is the difference between reading a novel and reading it critically — asking what the text does, not only what it says, and having the French vocabulary to say so.

That vocabulary is not optional decoration. It is the language of the French commentaire composé from lesson 16, of every French literature class, and of the DALF C2 papers on the lettres et sciences humaines track.

👁️ La Focalisation — Who Sees?

The first question to ask of any passage, and the one that changes the most.

TypeWhat the reader getsSignal
focalisation zéroeverything — the narrator knows all mindsElle ignorait qu’il l’attendait déjà.
focalisation interneonly what one character perceivesIl lui sembla entendre des pas.
focalisation externeonly what a camera could recordL’homme entra, s’assit, ne dit rien.

Ask the diagnostic question: can the narrator tell me what this character is thinking? If yes for everyone, focalisation zéro. If yes for one only, interne. If for nobody, externe. And notice what each choice hides — external focalisation makes a character opaque and is the standard device of suspense.

🗣️ Le Style Indirect Libre

The single most important technique in the French novel, and the one learners read straight past. A character's thought or speech is reported without que, without quotation marks and without a verb of speech — so the narrator's voice and the character's voice merge.

TypeExample
DirectIl pensa : « Je suis perdu. »
IndirectIl pensa qu’il était perdu.
Indirect libreIl était perdu. Comment avait-il pu en arriver là ?

In the third version, whose question is it? Both, at once — and that ambiguity is the point. The device lets a narrator inhabit a character while retaining the possibility of irony, which is precisely what Flaubert does to Emma Bovary for four hundred pages. Look for imperfects plus exclamations, questions or spoken vocabulary with no attribution.

💡 How to spot it

Three signals together: a shift to the imparfait, a question or exclamation that the narrator would have no reason to ask, and vocabulary belonging to the character rather than the narrator. Then ask: could I put quotation marks round this and a pensa-t-il after it? If yes, it is indirect libre.

✅ How to read a novel at C1 without exhausting yourself

Read a whole chapter without stopping, then go back and look up only the words that blocked comprehension — usually four or five, not forty. Looking up everything turns a novel into a vocabulary list and kills the one thing extensive reading gives you, which is speed.

📐 Writing the Analysis

The formula from lesson 16, applied: procédé + citation + effet, in one sentence, every time.

La métaphore filée de la prison (« les murs », « enfermée », « la grille ») traduit l’enfermement domestique d’Emma sans que la narratrice ait à le nommer.

Device named, evidence quoted, effect stated. Three sentences like that make a paragraph; four paragraphs make an axe de lecture. What does not work is naming devices without effects (a list) or describing effects without evidence (an impression).

🇫🇷 France

France teaches literary analysis as a technical discipline from the age of fifteen, with a fixed vocabulary and fixed exercises — the commentaire composé, the dissertation littéraire, the explication de texte — and the baccalauréat de français examines all three. One consequence worth knowing: an educated French reader will discuss a novel in terms of procédés and registres as a matter of course, and expects an interlocutor to do the same.

🇨🇦 Canada

Québec teaches the same toolkit — the épreuve uniforme de français at the end of cégep is a literary analysis — but the canon is its own: Gabrielle Roy, Anne Hébert, Michel Tremblay (whose Les Belles-Sœurs put joual on stage in 1968 and changed Québécois literature), Réjean Ducharme, Dany Laferrière, Wajdi Mouawad. Tremblay is the key text for this course's purposes: it is literature written deliberately in the spoken register of lesson 9.

Exercise 1 — Which focalisation?

Write zéro, interne or externe.

  1. « Elle ignorait qu’il l’attendait déjà. » → _____ (Answer: zéro|zero)
  2. « Il lui sembla entendre des pas. » → _____ (Answer: interne)
  3. « L’homme entra, s’assit, ne dit rien. » → _____ (Answer: externe)
  4. Suspense is most easily built with _____ focalisation. (Answer: externe)

Exercise 2 — Which kind of reported speech?

Write direct, indirect or libre.

  • Il pensa : « Je suis perdu. » → _____ (Answer: direct)
  • Il pensa qu’il était perdu. → _____ (Answer: indirect)
  • Il était perdu. Comment avait-il pu en arriver là ? → _____ (Answer: libre)
  • The tense that signals style indirect libre most often is the _____. (Answer: imparfait)

Exercise 3 — Modernise the archaism

  1. naguère → _____ (Answer: récemment|recemment)
  2. quérir → _____ (Answer: chercher)
  3. céans → _____ (Answer: ici)
  4. moult → _____ (Answer: beaucoup)
  5. qu’il vînt → qu’il _____ (Answer: vienne)

Exercise 4 — The critical vocabulary

  • A cluster of words on one theme is le champ _____. (Answer: lexical)
  • An extended metaphor is une métaphore _____. (Answer: filée|filee)
  • A story inside the story is un récit _____. (Answer: enchâssé|enchasse)
  • The opening of a novel is l’_____. (Answer: incipit)
  • The formula for an analytical sentence is procédé + citation + _____. (Answer: effet)
