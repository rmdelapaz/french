🇫🇷 Lesson 1: Le Subjonctif sans Limites

At B1 you learned the subjunctive as a list: these verbs, these conjunctions, this mood. That list is finished and you own it. What nobody told you is that the list is only the compulsory half of the subjunctive. The other half is a choice — and at C1 the choice carries meaning. Je connais quelqu’un qui parle russe and Je cherche quelqu’un qui parle russe use different moods and describe different worlds.

This lesson closes the mood system for good: the subjunctive as a semantic decision, the full concessive paradigm, and the two literary past subjunctives you met at B2 as a reader — now as a writer.

🎯 The Subjunctive as a Choice, Not a Trigger

Three places where French leaves the mood up to you, and the difference is real.

1. In a relative clause

The mood tells your listener whether the thing you are describing exists.

Indicative — it existsSubjunctive — it is sought or doubted
Je connais un avocat qui parle arabe.Je cherche un avocat qui parle arabe.
J’ai trouvé une maison qui a un jardin.Je voudrais une maison qui ait un jardin.
Il y a une solution qui convient.Y a-t-il une solution qui convienne ?

Two close relatives behave the same way. After a negative or restricted antecedent, the subjunctive is the norm: Il n’y a personne qui sache la réponse. And after a superlative or an exclusive (le seul, le premier, l’unique), the subjunctive marks the claim as an opinion rather than a measurement: C’est le seul film qui m’ait vraiment marqué.

💡 The test that works

Ask yourself: could I point at it? If yes, indicative. If you are describing a specification rather than a thing — something wanted, required, denied or ranked by feeling — subjunctive.

2. After le fait que

Both moods are correct, and educated speakers use both. The indicative asserts the fact; the subjunctive holds it at arm’s length, treating it as something being weighed rather than something being reported.

Le fait qu’il a démissionné a surpris tout le monde. — He resigned; that is established.
Le fait qu’il ait démissionné ne change rien. — Taking his resignation as a given, it changes nothing.

When le fait que opens the sentence, the subjunctive is markedly more common — the clause is being set up as a premise, not delivered as news.

3. De sorte que, de façon que — purpose or result?

SenseMoodExample
Purpose — so that it may happensubjunctiveParlez fort, de sorte que tout le monde entende.
Result — and so it did happenindicativeIl a parlé fort, de sorte que tout le monde a entendu.

Après que deserves a footnote. The prescriptive rule demands the indicative, because the event has happened. Educated speakers say the subjunctive constantly, by analogy with avant que. Write après qu’il est parti; expect to hear après qu’il soit parti; never correct anyone about it out loud.

🧩 The Concessive Paradigm

This is the block of structures that trips up every advanced learner, because four of them look almost identical and mean different things. Learn them as a table, once.

FrenchPronunciationMeaning & pattern

⚠️ quoi que vs quoique — the one-second test

Replace it with bien que. If the sentence still works, you want the single word quoique. If you need peu importe ce que instead, you want the two words quoi que.

Quoi qu’il arrive = whatever happens. Quoiqu’il soit malade = although he is ill.

Agreement in quel que. This is not a fixed phrase — quel is an adjective and agrees with the subject that follows soit:

Quel que soit le prix… / Quelle que soit la raison… / Quels que soient les risques… / Quelles que soient vos intentions…

🏛️ The Literary Past Subjunctives — Now as a Writer

At B2 you learned to recognise fût, vînt and eût. At C1 two uses become available to you in formal writing, because in these cases modern French offers no equally compact alternative.

1. Concessive inversion. The subjunctive imperfect, inverted, with no conjunction at all:

Fût-il président, il devrait répondre. — Even were he president, he would have to answer.
Dût-il en souffrir, il dira la vérité. — Even if he should suffer for it, he will tell the truth.

2. The unreal past in high register. Where ordinary French writes s’il avait su, il serait venu, very formal French can write l’eût-il su, il fût venu. You will meet this in judgments, in the Académie’s own prose and in nineteenth-century novels. Produce it only when you are deliberately writing in that register — otherwise it reads as costume.

🌟 If you want to go further

Track down a French Conseil d’État ruling or an avis of the Académie française online and count the subjunctives in the first paragraph. You will find relative-clause subjunctives, quel que agreements and at least one fût-il. The register is extreme, which is exactly why it makes the patterns visible.

🎚️ How Good French Still Dodges the Subjunctive

B1 lesson 7 taught you la fuite élégante. At C1 it is worth restating, because the temptation now runs the other way: having mastered the mood, learners over-use it. Natives still avoid it whenever a noun or an infinitive is available.

SubjunctiveWhat a native is more likely to write
avant qu’il ne parteavant son départ
bien qu’il soit fatiguémalgré sa fatigue
pour que nous comprenionspour nous permettre de comprendre
il faut que tu le fassestu dois le faire / à toi de le faire

A C1 writer chooses. The subjunctive is a tool, not a badge.

🇫🇷 France

School grammar in France still teaches the full four-tense subjunctive, and the imparfait du subjonctif appears in the agrégation and in legal drafting. In everyday Parisian speech the present subjunctive is alive and frequent — il faut que j’y aille — while the past subjunctive is noticeably rarer in speech than in writing. The prescriptive rule on après que is defended in newspaper language columns roughly once a year, and ignored the rest of the time.

🇨🇦 Canada

Québécois usage matches standard French in writing. In speech, the subjunctive is if anything more robust than in France after verbs of volition, and faut que + subjunctive is extremely frequent (faut que j’y aille, faut qu’on se parle). One distinctive pattern: Québécois speech readily uses the subjunctive after croire in the negative and in questions where France would keep the indicative. The literary fût-il forms belong to written Québécois exactly as they do in France.

Exercise 1 — Indicative or subjunctive in the relative clause?

Give the correct form of the verb in brackets.

  1. Je cherche une secrétaire qui _____ le russe. (parler) (Answer: parle)
  2. Je connais une secrétaire qui _____ le russe. (parler) (Answer: parle)
  3. Il n’y a personne ici qui _____ conduire. (savoir) (Answer: sache)
  4. C’est le seul argument qui m’_____ convaincu. (avoir) (Answer: ait)
  5. J’ai enfin trouvé un appartement qui me _____. (convenir) (Answer: convient)
  6. Connaissez-vous un logiciel qui _____ faire cela ? (pouvoir) (Answer: puisse)

Exercise 2 — quoi que, quoique, quel(le)(s) que

One word or two, and with the right agreement.

  • _____ tu fasses, fais-le bien. (Answer: quoi que)
  • _____ ce soit cher, je le prends. (Answer: quoique)
  • _____ soient les conséquences, je refuse. (Answer: quelles que)
  • _____ soit le prix, nous paierons. (Answer: quel que)
  • _____ il en dise, il a tort. (Answer: quoi qu)

Exercise 3 — Purpose or result?

Complete with the right mood after de sorte que.

  1. Range tes affaires, de sorte que la chambre _____ propre. (être) (Answer: soit)
  2. Il a tout rangé, de sorte que la chambre _____ propre. (être — result) (Answer: est)
  3. Parle plus fort, de sorte qu’on t’_____. (entendre) (Answer: entende)
  4. Il a crié, de sorte que tout le monde l’_____ entendu. (avoir) (Answer: a)

Exercise 4 — Formal register

Fill the blank with the missing word of each formal structure.

  • Venez, ne _____-ce que pour une heure. (Answer: fût|fut)
  • _____ supposer qu’il accepte, le problème reste entier. (Answer: à|a)
  • Pour _____ que je sache, le dossier est clos. (Answer: autant)
  • _____-il ministre, il devrait se justifier. (Answer: fût|fut)
  • Je décline — _____ que ce soit impossible, mais je manque de temps. (Answer: non)
