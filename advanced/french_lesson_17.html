🇫🇷 Lesson 17: La Langue Juridique & Administrative

Sooner or later, living in French means signing something. A bail, an employment contract, a set of conditions générales, a form from the préfecture. The language of those documents is deliberately archaic, heavily impersonal, and — once you know its fifty fixed expressions — remarkably predictable. It is not obscure for the sake of obscurity: it is obscure because every ambiguity has been litigated out of it.

This lesson gives you the reading skill and the two letters you will actually need to write.

💡 Two that change the meaning of a clause entirely

Nonobstant = despite what any other clause says — it overrides. Sous réserve de = only if that other condition holds — it subordinates. Confusing them inverts the obligation. If a contract contains either word, read the sentence twice.

✉️ The Two Letters You Will Write

1. La mise en demeure

A dated, formal, evidenced demand — the step before litigation, and usually the step that solves the problem. Send it en recommandé avec accusé de réception.

ElementWording
Object lineObjet : mise en demeure de procéder à…
The factsPar contrat du 12 mars 2026, vous vous êtes engagé à…
The defaultOr, à ce jour, cette obligation n’a pas été respectée.
The demand and deadlineJe vous mets en demeure d’y procéder dans un délai de quinze jours à compter de la réception de la présente.
The consequenceÀ défaut, je me réserve le droit de saisir la juridiction compétente.
CloseVeuillez agréer, Madame, Monsieur, l’expression de mes salutations distinguées.

2. Le recours gracieux

A polite request that an administration reconsider its own decision, before any tribunal is involved. The register is respectful and the argument is factual.

Par décision du 3 avril 2026, dont copie ci-jointe, vous avez rejeté ma demande de… Je me permets de solliciter le réexamen de cette décision au motif que… Je reste à votre disposition pour tout complément d’information.

⚠️ Formula discipline

The closing formula is fixed and hierarchical. To an administration or an unknown recipient: Veuillez agréer, Madame, Monsieur, l’expression de mes salutations distinguées. To someone senior: …l’expression de ma considération distinguée. Never Cordialement in a formal letter, and never Veuillez agréer mes sentiments to a woman from a man — the conventional restriction is real and still noted.

📰 Le Journal Officiel

French law is published in the JO, and the hierarchy of texts is worth knowing because it tells you how binding something is.

TextIssued byWeight
la loiParlementstatute
l’ordonnanceGouvernement, on delegationstatute-level
le décretPrésident or Premier ministreimplements a law
l’arrêtéministre, préfet, maireimplements a décret, locally or sectorally
la circulaireadministrationinternal instruction, not binding on citizens

Two maxims you will see quoted: Nul n’est censé ignorer la loi — nobody is deemed ignorant of the law — and Le silence de l’administration vaut acceptation, which since 2014 means that administrative silence after two months is, in many procedures, a tacit yes. Both are worth knowing in practice, not just as phrases.

🇫🇷 France

France's administrative language is famously dense, and the state has been trying to simplify it since the Comité pour la simplification du langage administratif of the 2000s — with partial success. Most procedures are now online at service-public.fr, which is also the best free source of model letters. The recommandé avec accusé de réception remains legally central: in a dispute, what matters is that you can prove the date of receipt.

🇨🇦 Canada

Québec's administrative vocabulary diverges sharply because the institutions differ and because Québec translates where France borrows. Key terms: the RAMQ (health insurance), the NAS (social insurance number), Revenu Québec, the CNESST (workplace health and safety), the TAL (rental tribunal, formerly the Régie du logement). Québec's langage clair policy also makes its official letters markedly shorter-sentenced and more verbal than French ones — a real and welcome difference.

Exercise 1 — Decode the legal connective

  1. despite any other clause → _____ (Answer: nonobstant)
  2. subject to → sous _____ de (Answer: réserve|reserve)
  3. hereby → par la _____ (Answer: présente|presente)
  4. the said contract → _____ contrat (Answer: ledit)
  5. where applicable → le cas _____ (Answer: échéant|echeant)
  6. with effect from → à _____ du (Answer: compter)

Exercise 2 — Who is who in a lease?

  • The landlord is le _____. (Answer: bailleur)
  • The tenant is le _____. (Answer: locataire|preneur)
  • The notice period is le _____. (Answer: préavis|preavis)
  • The inventory of condition is l’état des _____. (Answer: lieux)
  • The clause that ends the lease automatically is la clause _____. (Answer: résolutoire|resolutoire)

  2. Your bank details → _____ (Answer: RIB)
  3. Proof that you filed an application → _____ (Answer: récépissé|recepisse)
  4. Your tax assessment → _____ (Answer: avis d’imposition|avis d'imposition)

Exercise 4 — The hierarchy of texts

  • Voted by Parliament: la _____ (Answer: loi)
  • Signed by the Prime Minister to implement a law: le _____ (Answer: décret|decret)
  • Issued by a mayor or a prefect: l’_____ (Answer: arrêté|arrete)
  • An internal instruction that does not bind citizens: la _____ (Answer: circulaire)
