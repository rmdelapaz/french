🇫🇷 Lesson 10: Atténuation, Implicite & Politesse

French politeness is not built out of please and sorry. It is built out of grammar: the conditional, negation, the impersonal, and a repertoire of hedges that make a demanding request easy to refuse. A learner who translates English courtesy word for word comes out either brusque (je veux parler au directeur) or oddly servile (je suis très désolé de vous déranger, s’il vous plaît, excusez-moi).

This lesson is the middle path: how to soften, how to understate, how to refuse, complain and reproach without damage — and how to hear what was meant rather than what was said.

💡 The conditional is the politeness engine

Almost every softened structure in French runs through the conditional you learned at B1: j’aurais une question, il faudrait peut-être, vous pourriez éventuellement, je me permettrais de suggérer. When in doubt, put the verb in the conditional and the sentence becomes polite.

🎭 La Litote — Saying Less, Meaning More

Understatement through negation is a genuine feature of French, not an eccentricity. Ce n’est pas mal is praise. Corneille's Va, je ne te hais point is a declaration of love.

The trap for an English speaker is hearing these as lukewarm. Pas mal du tout from a French colleague about your work is a compliment; treat it as one.

🙅 Refusing

A French refusal characteristically names an external constraint rather than a preference. I don't want to is an escalation; I can't, because X is a refusal.

Je crains que ce ne soit difficile.
Ça ne va pas être possible, malheureusement.
J’aurais aimé, mais je suis déjà pris.
Écoutez, je vais être franc avec vous… — announces a direct refusal, which is itself a courtesy.
Je préférerais éviter, si cela ne vous ennuie pas.

⚠️ Non is not rude

French tolerates open disagreement far better than English does. Non, je ne suis pas d’accord in a meeting is normal participation, and disagreeing with someone is often taken as taking them seriously. What is rude is not the non — it is refusing to engage with the argument.

😠 Complaining and Reproaching

The complaint formula: factual account, consequence, expected remedy. Emotion is carried by the register, not by adjectives.

Je me permets de vous signaler que la commande n’est toujours pas arrivée. Cela nous met dans une situation délicate. Pourriez-vous me confirmer une date ?

For reproach, French prefers the conditional past or a question over an accusation:

Direct (avoid)What to say instead
Tu ne m’as pas prévenu !Tu aurais pu me prévenir…
C’est de ta faute.On aurait peut-être dû s’organiser autrement.
Vous avez oublié.Il semblerait que cela soit passé à la trappe.
Vous êtes en retard.Nous vous attendions un peu plus tôt.

🤝 Negotiating tu and vous

At A1 you learned the distinction. At C1 the interesting part is the transition, which is a small social ritual.

  • It is offered, not assumed — usually by the older, more senior or longer-established person: On peut se tutoyer ? · Tutoyons-nous, c’est plus simple.
  • You may decline gracefully by simply continuing with vous; nobody comments.
  • Mixed use is normal — vous to a client and tu to the colleague beside you, in the same conversation.
  • Reverting from tu to vous is a deliberate cooling and will be read as one.
  • Sector matters more than age: start-ups, sport and activism tutoie immediately; banking, law and administration do not.

🕵️ Reading the Implicit

French conversation leaves a good deal unsaid, and answering the literal words instead of the implication is a classic advanced-learner slip.

What is saidWhat is meant

On verra.Probably not.
Je vais voir ce que je peux faire.No promises at all.
Ce n’est pas évident.It is very difficult.

Il faudrait qu’on en reparle.Not now, possibly never.
Vous n’êtes pas sans savoir que…You certainly know that — and I am reminding you pointedly.

🇫🇷 France

French politeness is formal at the openings and closings and direct in the middle: the greeting ritual (bonjour before anything else, in every shop) is non-negotiable, but disagreement inside the conversation is expected. Vouvoiement remains the default with strangers and in most workplaces, and the generational shift towards tu is real but slower than in Québec. Omitting bonjour is a far worse offence than saying non.

🇨🇦 Canada

Québec tutoies much earlier and much more widely — in shops, by phone, and often with a first-time client — and vous can read as cold or as distance-creating. Québécois politeness is also warmer at the surface and more conflict-avoidant than French: open contradiction in a meeting is less common, and softeners are used more heavily. Where a French colleague says je ne suis pas d'accord, a Québécois one is likelier to say je verrais ça autrement.

✍️ Practice

Exercise 1 — Soften it

Complete the hedged version.

  1. C’est faux. → Il me _____ que ce soit inexact. (Answer: semble)
  2. Je veux une réponse. → J’_____ besoin d’une réponse. (conditional) (Answer: aurais)
  3. Vous avez tort. → Sauf _____ de ma part, ce n’est pas le cas. (Answer: erreur)
  4. Donnez-moi ce dossier. → _____-vous la gentillesse de me transmettre ce dossier ? (Answer: auriez)
  5. C’est impossible. → Je _____ que ce ne soit difficile. (Answer: crains)

Exercise 2 — Decode the litote

Write positif or négatif for the real meaning.

  • Ce n’est pas mal ! → _____ (Answer: positif)
  • Ce n’est pas la joie. → _____ (Answer: négatif|negatif)
  • Il n’est pas bête. → _____ (Answer: positif)
  • Ce n’est pas rien. → _____ (Answer: positif)

Exercise 3 — Reproach without accusing

Complete the softer version.

  1. Tu ne m’as pas prévenu ! → Tu _____ pu me prévenir. (Answer: aurais)
  2. Vous avez oublié. → Il _____ que cela soit passé à la trappe. (Answer: semblerait)
  3. Vous êtes en retard. → Nous vous _____ un peu plus tôt. (imparfait) (Answer: attendions)
  4. C’est de ta faute. → On aurait peut-être _____ s’organiser autrement. (Answer: dû|du)

Exercise 4 — What is actually meant?

Write oui or non for the real answer.

  • On verra. → _____ (Answer: non)
  • C’est une idée intéressante… → do they like it? _____ (Answer: non)
  • Je ne dis pas non. → _____ (Answer: oui)
  • Il faudrait qu’on en reparle. → is it happening soon? _____ (Answer: non)
