🇫🇷 Lesson 5: Les Pronoms à la Limite

You can already say je le lui donne. This lesson is about the cases where that competence runs out: two pronouns plus an infinitive, pronouns with the causative faire, the neuter le that stands for a whole clause, celui with its obligatory tail, soi, and the fixed verbs where y and en have stopped meaning anything at all.

None of it is hard. All of it is the kind of thing that, done wrong, makes fluent French sound assembled rather than spoken.

Read the table left to right and you have the order. Two consequences catch everyone:

Third person reverses the intuition. Je le lui donne — not je lui le donne. But with a first or second person indirect object, the indirect comes first: Il me le donne. The rule is the column order, not the grammatical function.

Column 1 and column 3 never co-occur. You cannot say il me lui présente. French repairs this with a stressed pronoun: Il me présente à lui.

📍 Where the Pronouns Go

StructurePlacementExample
Simple tensebefore the verbJe le lui donne.
Compound tensebefore the auxiliaryJe le lui ai donné.
Verb + infinitivebefore the infinitiveJe vais le lui donner.
Negativeinside the ne… pasJe ne le lui donne pas.
Positive imperativeafter, hyphenated, direct firstDonne-le-lui !
Negative imperativenormal order, before the verbNe le lui donne pas !
Causative fairebefore faire, not the infinitiveJe le lui fais lire.

⚠️ The imperative flips the order

Donne-le-moi — direct object first, and me becomes moi. But make it negative and everything returns to normal: Ne me le donne pas.

With the causative, the pronouns attach to faire and never to the infinitive: Je les ai fait venir (and see lesson 7 — fait stays invariable there).

⚪ The Neuter le

This le does not replace a noun. It replaces a clause, an adjective or a whole idea — and because it is not a noun, it never agrees.

Elle est malade ? — Je le sais.
Je te l’avais dit ! — I told you so.
Elles sont fatiguées, et elles le resteront. — invariable, though the subject is feminine plural.
Si vous le souhaitez… — if you wish (formal fixed use).

Compare Tu connais la réponse ? — Oui, je la connais (a noun) with Tu sais qu’il part ? — Oui, je le sais (a clause).

👉 Celui Cannot Stand Alone

Celui, celle, ceux, celles always need a tail: de + noun, a relative pronoun, or -ci / -là.

Note the last line: in French, celui-ci = the latter (the nearer one) and celui-là = the former. English orders them the other way round in prose, which makes this a genuine reading trap.

🧍 Soi

Soi is the stressed pronoun for an indefinite subject — on, chacun, personne, tout le monde, or an infinitive.

Chacun pour soi. · On rentre chez soi. · Il faut avoir confiance en soi. · Cela va de soi.

With a definite subject you use lui, elle, eux: Paul est rentré chez lui — never chez soi here.

🌟 If you want to go further

Take any page of a French novel and circle every y and en. Sort them into three piles: real pronouns with a traceable antecedent, fossilised verbs from the list above, and en as a partitive quantity. The proportions will surprise you — the fossils win.

🇫🇷 France

Spoken French simplifies this system quietly. Lui and leur are frequently replaced by à lui / à eux after the verb, and clusters of three pronouns are avoided by restructuring. Il y en a reduces to y en a and often to yan-a. In careful speech and in all writing, the table above is followed exactly.

🇨🇦 Canada

Québécois speech has two well-known features here. Y serves as a reduced form of lui and il(s) (j'y ai dit for je lui ai dit), which is stigmatised in writing but extremely common orally. And chez nous can mean chez moi — a first-person plural used for a single speaker's home, with no equivalent in France. Written Québécois follows the standard order without exception.

✍️ Practice

Exercise 1 — Order the pronouns

Rewrite with both pronouns in the right order.

  1. Je donne le livre à Paul. → Je _____ donne. (Answer: le lui)
  2. Il rend la clé à moi. → Il _____ rend. (Answer: me la)
  3. Elle parle du projet à ses parents. → Elle _____ parle. (Answer: leur en)
  4. Nous envoyons les photos à vous. → Nous _____ envoyons. (Answer: vous les)
  5. Il y a trois solutions. → Il _____ a trois. (Answer: y en)

Exercise 2 — Placement

  • Je vais dire cela à Marie. → Je vais _____ dire. (Answer: le lui)
  • Donne le livre à moi ! → Donne-_____ ! (Answer: le-moi)
  • Ne donne pas le livre à lui ! → Ne _____ donne pas ! (Answer: le lui)
  • J’ai fait lire ce texte à mes élèves. → Je _____ ai fait lire. (Answer: le leur)

Exercise 3 — Neuter le or agreeing pronoun?

  1. Tu sais qu’elle part ? — Oui, je _____ sais. (Answer: le)
  2. Tu connais la réponse ? — Oui, je _____ connais. (Answer: la)
  3. Elles sont prêtes et elles _____ resteront. (Answer: le)
  4. Vous voulez les billets ? — Oui, je _____ veux. (Answer: les)

Exercise 4 — celui, soi and the fossils

  • Mon vélo est cassé ; je prends _____ de ma sœur. (Answer: celui)
  • Chacun pour _____. (Answer: soi)
  • Paul est rentré chez _____. (Answer: lui)
  • Je n’_____ peux plus de ce bruit. (Answer: en)
  • Il _____ connaît en informatique. (Answer: s’y|s'y)
