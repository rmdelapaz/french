🇫🇷 Lesson 2: La Phrase Longue & la Mise en Relief

Here is a sentence from a French weekly. Read it once, out loud.

Si l’on en croit les chiffres publiés mardi par l’institut, dont la méthodologie a pourtant été contestée à plusieurs reprises, la baisse observée depuis janvier, loin de traduire un redressement durable, ne serait qu’un effet mécanique de la révision des seuils.

Forty-six words, one main verb. English journalism would have written three sentences. French did not, and a C1 reader has to be able to take a sentence like that apart in real time — and then build one on purpose.

This lesson has two halves: parsing (how to read a long French sentence) and emphasis (how French decides what a sentence is about, given that it cannot stress a word the way English can).

🔍 Parsing: Find the Main Verb First

English marks emphasis with the voice: I saw HIM yesterday. French cannot do that nearly as freely, so it moves things instead. The consequence for reading is that the core of a French sentence is often buried in the middle. The fix is a fixed procedure.

StepWhat you look for
1Bracket every si / dont / qui / que / parce que clause — everything inside is scaffolding.
2Bracket every pair of commas: an incise is almost always removable.
3What is left is subject + main verb. Read those two words alone.
4Now put the scaffolding back, one bracket at a time.

Applied to the sentence above: strip Si l’on en croit… l’institut, strip dont la méthodologie… reprises, strip loin de traduire… durable, and what remains is la baisse ne serait qu’un effet mécanique. Eight words. Everything else qualifies them.

💡 The one-sentence version

French subordinates where English coordinates. Where an English writer puts a full stop and starts again, a French writer puts a comma and a relative pronoun. That is a stylistic preference, not a grammatical obligation — but reading French means expecting it.

⭐ Mise en Relief: The Cleft

French has one dominant device for emphasis, and it is worth over-learning: c’est… qui for subjects, c’est… que for everything else.

NeutralEmphasisedWhat is highlighted
Marie a payé l’addition.C’est Marie qui a payé l’addition.the subject
Marie a payé l’addition.C’est l’addition que Marie a payée.the object
Il est parti hier.C’est hier qu’il est parti.the time
Je te parle de ça.C’est de ça que je te parle.the complement

Two things to notice. First, the cleft triggers participle agreement with a preceding direct object — l’addition que Marie a payée, exactly as B1 lesson 10 taught. Second, c’est stays singular in speech even before a plural (c’est eux qui ont décidé), although ce sont eux qui is the written standard.

The pseudo-cleft: announcing the frame first

A step up in sophistication. Instead of highlighting a word, you announce the kind of thing you are about to say, then say it.

Ce que je reproche à ce projet, c’est son coût.
Ce qui m’inquiète, c’est le calendrier.
Ce dont nous avons besoin, c’est d’un vrai débat.
Ce à quoi personne ne s’attendait, c’est sa démission.

Choose the relative by the preposition the verb needs: reprocher qqch → ce que; avoir besoin de → ce dont; s’attendre à → ce à quoi. Getting this right is one of the clearest single markers of a C1 speaker.

🔄 Dislocation: The Spoken Cleft

Speech does the same job more cheaply, by naming the topic outside the clause and leaving a pronoun behind.

FrenchPronunciationMeaning
Moi, je trouve ça bizarre.mwah zhuh troov sah bee-ZAHRPersonally, I find that odd.
Ce film, je l’ai déjà vu.suh feelm zhuh lay day-zhah VÜThat film, I have already seen it.
Mon frère, il habite à Lyon.mohn frair eel ah-beet ah lee-OHNMy brother lives in Lyon.
Il est où, ton sac ?eel ay OO tohn SAKWhere is your bag?
Le fromage, j’en mange peu.luh fro-mahzh zhahn mahnzh PUHCheese, I eat little of it.

Dislocation is not careless French. It is the standard spoken topic-marking device, and a whole conversation can run on it. Lesson 9 takes it further; here, notice only that the dislocated element is resumed by a pronoun — ce film, je l’ai vu, never ce film, j’ai vu.

↩️ Inversion After a Fronted Adverb

Certain adverbs, when they open a clause, pull the subject after the verb. This is written French, and it is unavoidable in the press.

FrenchPronunciationMeaning
Aussi devons-nous agir vite.oh-see duh-vohn noo ah-zheer VEETTherefore we must act quickly.
Peut-être a-t-il raison.puh-tetr ah-teel ray-ZOHNPerhaps he is right.
Sans doute avait-elle peur.sahn doot ah-vay-tel PUHRNo doubt she was afraid.
À peine était-il sorti que…ah pen ay-tay-teel sor-TEE kuhHardly had he gone out when…
Encore faut-il le prouver.ahn-kor foh-teel luh proo-VAYEven so, it has to be proved.
Ainsi s’achève le débat.an-see sah-shev luh day-BAHThus the debate ends.

⚠️ Aussi has two lives

In the middle of a clause, aussi means also: Je viens aussi. At the head of a clause with inversion, it means therefore: Aussi ai-je refusé. The position changes the word's meaning, not just its emphasis.

If you would rather not invert, the everyday alternatives are peut-être qu’il a raison and c’est pourquoi j’ai refusé. Both are perfectly good French; they are simply a rung lower on the register ladder.

🏗️ Building One on Purpose

Take four short statements and assemble them into one controlled sentence.

Les prix ont baissé. La baisse date de janvier. L’institut a publié des chiffres. Sa méthodologie est contestée.

→ Les prix, dont la baisse date de janvier, auraient reculé selon des chiffres publiés par un institut à la méthodologie contestée.

The moves used: a relative with dont, a nominalisation (la baisse), a reduced participle clause (publiés par), and a prepositional qualifier (à la méthodologie contestée). Lesson 3 is entirely about that second move.

And the counter-advice: a long sentence must have one idea, however many clauses. If you cannot say what the main verb is doing, split it. French admires the long période; it does not admire confusion.

🇫🇷 France

The long periodic sentence is actively taught in France — the dissertation rewards subordination, and Le Monde's house style tolerates sentences that an English copy editor would break in three. Fronted aussi with inversion is standard editorial French. In speech, dislocation is so pervasive that a recorded Parisian conversation transcribed literally looks ungrammatical to learners who have only read the language.

🇨🇦 Canada

Written Québécois follows the same norms, but Québec's public-service writing has been through a strong langage clair movement, and government texts are noticeably shorter-sentenced than their French equivalents. Spoken Québécois dislocates at least as heavily as France, with its own favourite frames: moi là, je trouve que… and the tag -tu in yes/no questions (ça se peut-tu ?), which has no counterpart in France.

Exercise 1 — Find the main verb

Write only the main (finite, non-subordinate) verb of each sentence.

  1. Si tu as le temps, le dossier que je t’ai envoyé hier _____ ta relecture. (Answer: attend)
  2. Les chiffres, dont personne ne conteste la source, _____ clairs. (Answer: sont)
  3. Parce qu’il pleuvait, la réunion, prévue dehors, _____ annulée. (Answer: a été|fut)
  4. Ce que je voulais dire, avant qu’on m’interrompe, _____ simple. (Answer: était|etait)

Exercise 2 — Cleft the underlined element

Supply the missing qui or que.

  • C’est Paul _____ a téléphoné. (Answer: qui)
  • C’est Paul _____ j’ai appelé. (Answer: que)
  • C’est demain _____ nous partons. (Answer: que)
  • C’est de son avenir _____ il s’agit. (Answer: que)
  • Ce sont mes voisins _____ ont tout organisé. (Answer: qui)

Exercise 3 — Pseudo-cleft: choose the relative

Use ce que, ce qui, ce dont or ce à quoi.

  1. _____ me gêne, c’est le bruit. (Answer: ce qui)
  2. _____ j’ai besoin, c’est de calme. (Answer: ce dont)
  3. _____ tu dis est faux. (Answer: ce que)
  4. _____ je pense, c’est à ma famille. (Answer: ce à quoi)
  5. _____ elle rêve, c’est d’un long voyage. (Answer: ce dont)

Exercise 4 — Invert after the fronted adverb

Complete the inverted form.

  • Peut-être _____-il raison. (avoir) (Answer: a-t)
  • Aussi _____-nous refuser. (devoir) (Answer: devons)
  • À peine _____-elle arrivée que le téléphone a sonné. (être) (Answer: était|etait)
  • Encore _____-il le prouver. (falloir) (Answer: faut)
