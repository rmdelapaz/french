🇫🇷 Lesson 21: La Poésie Française : Versification

French verse does not count stresses. It counts syllables — and since the number of syllables in a French line depends on whether you pronounce the unstable e, the whole system rests on the e caduc you studied in lesson 8. That connection is why this lesson sits here rather than being decoration: French metrics is applied phonology.

Twelve syllables, a break after the sixth, and a rhyme scheme. Once you can count a line, French poetry from Ronsard to Césaire opens up — and reading it aloud correctly stops being guesswork.

🔢 Counting Syllables: the Two Rules

Rule 1 — a final e counts as a syllable before a consonant, and is silent before a vowel or at the end of the line.

Un1 e2-tran3-ge4 bai5-ser6 — the e of étrange counts, because baiser begins with a consonant.

Take Baudelaire's line and count it:

La na-ture est un tem-ple où de vi-vants pi-liers
La(1) na(2) tu(3) r’est(4) un(5) tem(6) — pl’où(7) de(8) vi(9) vants(10) pi(11) liers(12)

Twelve. An alexandrine. Notice that the e of nature elides into est, and the e of temple into où — both before vowels, both silent.

Rule 2 — diérèse and synérèse. A vowel sequence like -ion or -ien may count as one syllable or two, and the poet chooses whichever makes the count work.

FrenchPronunciationEffect
une diérèseün dyay-REZsplits into two: pas-si-on (3 syllables)
une synérèseün see-nay-REZkeeps as one: pas-sion (2 syllables)
un hiatusuhn ee-ah-TÜStwo vowels meeting across words, classically avoided
une élisionün ay-lee-ZYOHNthe silent e before a vowel

✂️ La Césure

The classical alexandrine breaks after the sixth syllable into two hémistiches. The break is a syntactic pause, not just a count:

Je fais souvent ce rêve // étrange et pénétrant (6 // 6)

The Romantics broke the rule deliberately, and knowing that makes the effect visible. Victor Hugo's famous boast was j’ai disloqué ce grand niais d’alexandrin — he shifted the caesura, producing the trimètre romantique in three groups of four:

J’ai disloqué // ce grand niais // d’alexandrin (4 // 4 // 4)

FrenchPronunciationMeaning
un enjambementuhn ahn-zhahnb-MAHNthe sense runs on past the line break
un rejetuhn ruh-ZHAYa short spillover at the start of the next line
un contre-rejetuhn kohntr-ruh-ZHAYa short anticipation at the end of the previous line
un trimètreuhn tree-METRan alexandrine in three groups of four

An enjambement with a short rejet throws weight onto the spilt word, because the line break forces a micro-pause before it. That is the whole mechanism, and it is worth hearing: read the line aloud, pause at the break, and feel where the stress lands.

🔔 La Rime

French classifies rhyme twice: by richness (how many sounds are shared) and by arrangement.

FrenchPronunciationDefinition
une rime pauvreün reem POHVR1 shared sound — ami / joli
une rime suffisanteün reem sü-fee-ZAHNT2 shared sounds — ami / parti
une rime richeün reem REESH3 or more — image / hommage
des rimes platesday reem PLAHTAABB — couplets
des rimes croiséesday reem krwah-ZAYABAB — alternating
des rimes embrasséesday reem ahn-brah-SAYABBA — enclosed
une rime féminineün reem fay-mee-NEENending in a silent e
une rime masculineün reem mahs-kü-LEENnot ending in a silent e

Classical verse alternates feminine and masculine rhymes, and this is worth knowing because it explains spellings that otherwise look arbitrary in old editions — poets chose word forms to make the alternation work.

🧱 The Fixed Forms

FormShape
le sonnettwo quatrains + two tercets, usually alexandrines; the turn comes at the ninth line
la balladethree stanzas with a refrain plus an envoi
le rondeaua short fixed form built on a returning opening line
le poème en proseprose with poetic organisation — Baudelaire, Rimbaud
le calligrammethe text laid out as a picture — Apollinaire

The sonnet's volta — the turn at the start of the tercets — is the single most useful thing to look for when reading one. The quatrains set up a situation; the tercets change its meaning. If you can locate the turn, you have understood the poem's architecture even if some vocabulary escapes you.

Racine's line Pour qui sont ces serpents qui sifflent sur vos têtes ? is the school example of alliteration — six sibilants hissing. It is also an alexandrine: count it.

🌟 If you want to go further

Take the first quatrain of Baudelaire's Correspondances and mark it up completely: syllable count per line, caesura, rhyme scheme and richness, any diérèse. It will take twenty minutes and you will never read French verse the same way again. Then read it aloud, pronouncing every e the count requires — that is how the line was meant to sound.

🇫🇷 France

Versification is taught technically in French schools and examined at the baccalauréat: candidates are expected to scan a line and name its figures. The alexandrine remains culturally alive — in rap, in advertising slogans, in headlines — and French listeners hear it without being told. Slam and rap are now the main venue for metrical virtuosity, and artists such as Grand Corps Malade are read as part of the tradition rather than against it.

🇨🇦 Canada

Québécois poetry made a deliberate break with French metrics in the 1950s and 60s: Gaston Miron, Anne Hébert and the Hexagone generation worked in free verse and in a recognisably Québécois idiom, treating the classical alexandrine as a borrowed costume. The chanson tradition — Vigneault, Leclerc, Leloup — carries much of Québec's verse culture, and counting the e caduc matters there too, because Québécois pronunciation drops it differently from Parisian.

Exercise 2 — Does the final e count?

Write oui or non.

  • « une étrange beauté » — the e of étrange, before beauté → _____ (Answer: oui)
  • « une étrange idée » — the e of étrange, before idée → _____ (Answer: non)
  • the final e at the very end of a line → _____ (Answer: non)
  • « la nature est » — the e of nature → _____ (Answer: non)

Exercise 3 — Rhyme

  1. ABAB is called rimes _____ (Answer: croisées|croisees)
  2. AABB is called rimes _____ (Answer: plates)
  3. ABBA is called rimes _____ (Answer: embrassées|embrassees)
  4. image / hommage shares three sounds, so it is a rime _____ (Answer: riche)
  5. ami / joli shares one sound, so it is a rime _____ (Answer: pauvre)

Exercise 4 — Structure and figures

  • The half-line of an alexandrine is an _____ (Answer: hémistiche|hemistiche)
  • The break after the sixth syllable is la _____ (Answer: césure|cesure)
  • Sense running past the line break is un _____ (Answer: enjambement)
  • A sonnet has two quatrains and two _____ (Answer: tercets)
  • Repeated consonant sounds make une _____ (Answer: allitération|alliteration)
