🇫🇷 Lesson 19: Rhétorique, Discours Public & Langue de Bois

French public speech is built out of figures, and the audience hears them. A triple repetition, a chiasmus, a rhetorical question at the turn — these are not ornaments a French listener tolerates; they are the expected furniture of a speech, and their absence reads as flatness.

The same equipment works in reverse. Once you can name the devices, you can see them being used on you — in a minister's statement, in an editorial, in a press release that says nothing at all with great fluency. This lesson teaches both directions: how to build a persuasive French text, and how to take one apart.

💡 The tricolon is the workhorse

Three parallel elements, the third slightly longer. Liberté, égalité, fraternité. Je suis venu, je l’ai écouté, et j’ai changé d’avis. If you learn one rhetorical habit for French speech, make it this one — it sounds finished in a way two or four elements do not.

🏛️ Structuring a Discours

PartJobTypical opening
l’exordeestablish the occasion and your right to speakMesdames, Messieurs, chers collègues…
la narrationstate the facts as you see themIl y a deux ans, nous avons…
la confirmationthe argument proper, with concession and rebuttalCertes… mais il n’en demeure pas moins que…
la réfutationtake on the opposing case directlyOn nous dit que… Or les faits montrent…
la péroraisonlift and close, usually on the tricolonIl nous appartient donc de…

The engine inside la confirmation is the same concede-then-refute pair from lesson 4. In speech it has a standard spoken form: On nous dit que… Or… — you voice the objection in the opponent's words, then pivot with or. Done well, it makes you sound fair and leaves the objection dismantled.

🪵 La Langue de Bois

The most useful analytical skill on this page. Langue de bois is fluent, grammatical French that commits to nothing. It works by four mechanisms, all of which you already know:

🔎 The test that exposes it

Take any sentence and ask three questions: who does the action, what exactly happens, and when. If a fluent, confident sentence cannot answer all three, it is langue de bois — and the fix, if you are the one writing, is to put the agent back in and use a verb.

📰 Reading the Press for Slant

French journalism carries its position in small grammatical choices rather than in overt statements. Four places to look:

SignalNeutralLoaded
Verb of attributiona déclaré, a indiquéa prétendu, a reconnu, a lâché, a martelé
Journalistic conditionala démissionnéaurait démissionné (unverified)
Lexical choiceun mouvement socialune grogne, une fronde, un chaos
Quotation marks as distanceles réformesles « réformes »
What is omittedboth sides quotedone side quoted, the other summarised

Reconnaître is the sharpest of these: le ministre a reconnu que presupposes that the statement was reluctant and damaging. Prétendre signals that the writer disbelieves it. Neither is an opinion sentence, and both are opinions.

🗣️ Delivery

Everything from lesson 8 now becomes a performance skill.

  • Phrase in groups. Mark your text with slashes before you speak it: Mes chers collègues, / nous avons devant nous / une décision simple. Stress the final syllable of each group.
  • Slow at the turn. The transition between argument and rebuttal is where a French audience listens hardest; slow down and let the or land.
  • Use the accent d’insistance. First syllable of the word that matters: c’est INacceptable.
  • Liaise more than you would in conversation. Optional liaisons raise the register; a speech is the one place to make almost all of them.
  • Silence is punctuation. A two-second pause before the peroration does more than any adjective.

🌟 If you want to go further

Find the text and the recording of one famous French speech — Malraux's eulogy for Jean Moulin, Badinter's address on abolishing the death penalty, or Césaire at the Assemblée. Mark the figures on the page, then listen and mark the pauses. You will see the machinery of this lesson operating at full power, and Badinter in particular is a textbook of concession-then-refutation.

🇫🇷 France

France maintains a live rhetorical culture: éloquence competitions in law faculties, a recognised genre of the presidential address, and an audience that notices figures. Political langue de bois is itself a media topic, with journalists explicitly naming éléments de langage on air. The press is openly positioned — readers know where Le Figaro, Libération and L'Humanité stand — so slant is a matter of reading the house, not of detecting a hidden bias.

🇨🇦 Canada

Québécois public speech is generally plainer and less formally rhetorical than French — closer to North American norms, with shorter sentences and less classical staging — and audiences can hear heavy French-style rhetoric as pompous. Political language has its own euphemisms (une rationalisation des effectifs, un virage) and its own loaded vocabulary around language and sovereignty, where the choice between les Québécois, les Canadiens français and la nation is itself a position.
