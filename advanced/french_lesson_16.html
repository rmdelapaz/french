🇫🇷 Lesson 16: Le Français Académique

A French dissertation is not an English essay written in French. It has a different shape, a different opening ritual, and a different idea of what an argument is for. An excellent English essay submitted to a French examiner will be marked down — not for its French, but for hors sujet and absence de problématique.

This matters practically: the DALF C1 written paper is built on these genres, and so is every French university assignment. Three genres, one method each.

❓ La Problématique Comes First

The single most important concept here. A French essay does not answer a topic; it answers a question that you formulate out of the topic — a question containing a real tension.

Topic givenBad (a plan, not a problem)Good problématique
Le télétravailQuels sont les avantages du télétravail ?Le télétravail libère-t-il le salarié ou dissout-il la frontière entre vie professionnelle et vie privée ?
La lecture à l’ère numériqueComment lit-on aujourd’hui ?Le numérique transforme-t-il la lecture ou en menace-t-il la possibilité même ?
Le tourisme de masseLe tourisme est-il bon ?Peut-on préserver ce que le tourisme rend accessible ?

💡 The test for a problématique

If it can be answered oui or non in one sentence, it is not a problématique. It must contain a contradiction, a paradox or a tension that takes three sections to resolve. Everything else in the essay exists to answer it.

🏗️ The Three Plans

PlanShapeUse it when
dialectiquethèse → antithèse → synthèsethe subject is a debate — the default
thématiqueaspect 1 → aspect 2 → aspect 3the subject is broad and not oppositional
analytiqueconstat → causes → conséquences / solutionsthe subject is a problem to be explained

Two warnings about the dialectical plan, both of which examiners complain about every year. The antithèse is not "the disadvantages" — it is a genuinely different reading of the same question. And the synthèse is not a summary or a lukewarm compromise; it is a third position that reframes the question so that the first two stop contradicting each other.

📜 The Introduction Ritual

Four moves, in this order, in one paragraph. It is written last, once you know what you have argued.

MoveJobTypical wording
1. L’accrochea fact, a quotation, a current eventEn 2024, plus d’un salarié français sur trois…
2. Le contextedefine the terms, narrow the fieldPar télétravail, on entendra ici…
3. La problématiquethe question itselfDès lors, on peut se demander si…
4. L’annonce du planthe three sections, announcedNous verrons d’abord… avant d’examiner… pour enfin envisager…

The conclusion mirrors it: answer the problématique explicitly, summarise the route you took, then ouvrir — one sentence widening to a related question. Never introduce a new argument in the conclusion.

🔗 Transitions Are Marked

Between each major section, a French essay writes a visible bridge: close what you have shown, then open what it forces you to consider.

Si le télétravail offre bien cette liberté, encore faut-il se demander à quel prix. Or, les enquêtes récentes suggèrent…

Notice the machinery from lesson 4 doing the work: encore faut-il with its inversion, then or as the pivot. Transitions are where your connective inventory earns its keep.

📖 Le Commentaire Composé

The literary genre. You are given a text and must produce an organised interpretation.

DoDon’t
Organise by axes de lecture — two or three interpretive anglesWalk down the text line by line
Quote briefly and analyse the quotationParaphrase what the text says
Name the device and state its effectList devices without effects
Keep the author's intentions out of it unless evidencedSpeculate about the author's life

A usable formula for every analytical sentence: procédé + citation + effet. L’anaphore de « jamais » (l. 4-6) impose au lecteur le rythme d’une obsession. Device, evidence, effect — in one sentence. Lesson 20 supplies the vocabulary of devices, lesson 21 the vocabulary of verse.

🎓 The Academic Register

French academic prose is impersonal and hedged. Je pense que is not wrong but is weak; on, the passive and impersonal constructions carry the argument.

⚠️ Three things that lose marks regardless of language quality

Hors sujet — writing about the topic instead of answering the problématique. No plan announced — the examiner cannot see your structure. A synthèse that is a summary — it must add a position, not restate two.

📚 Citing

Short quotations run inside « » with the reference in brackets; longer ones are indented without quotation marks. Line references in a literary commentary use l. and v. for verse. Footnotes are common and expected, and ibid., op. cit. and cf. remain in normal use in French academic writing.

🇫🇷 France

The dissertation and commentaire composé are institutions, taught from the lycée through the agrégation, and the tripartite plan is close to compulsory. French marking rewards structure and conceptual rigour over originality of content, and a beautifully written essay with no problématique will fail. The note de synthèse of the administrative concours is a close cousin, and so is the DALF C1's synthesis task.

🇨🇦 Canada

Québec's universities sit between the two traditions: the cégep system still teaches the dissertation explicitly (the épreuve uniforme de français is one), but graduate writing follows North American norms much more closely — an explicit thesis statement up front, APA-style citation, less dialectical staging. If you write for a Québécois institution, ask which model is expected; the two are genuinely different and both are correct in their place.

✍️ Practice

Exercise 1 — Name the plan

Write dialectique, thématique or analytique.

  1. thèse / antithèse / synthèse → _____ (Answer: dialectique)
  2. constat / causes / conséquences → _____ (Answer: analytique)
  3. three aspects of a broad subject → _____ (Answer: thématique|thematique)
  4. The default plan for a debatable question is the _____ one. (Answer: dialectique)

Exercise 2 — The four moves of the introduction

Put the missing name of each move.

  • A striking fact or quotation to open: l’_____ (Answer: accroche)
  • Defining the terms: le _____ (Answer: contexte)
  • The central question: la _____ (Answer: problématique|problematique)
  • Announcing the sections: l’_____ du plan (Answer: annonce)

Exercise 3 — Academic formulas

  1. Il _____ de souligner que ce point est central. (Answer: convient)
  2. Tout _____ à croire que la tendance se poursuivra. (Answer: porte)
  3. On peut dès _____ avancer une autre hypothèse. (Answer: lors)
  4. Cette lecture mérite d’être _____. (Answer: nuancée|nuancee)
  5. _____ est de reconnaître que les données manquent. (Answer: force)

Exercise 4 — Commentaire composé

  • A commentaire is organised by _____ de lecture, not line by line. (Answer: axes)
  • The formula for an analytical sentence is procédé + citation + _____. (Answer: effet)
  • Writing about the topic instead of the question is called _____ sujet. (Answer: hors)
  • The conclusion should answer the problématique and then _____ on a wider question. (Answer: ouvrir)
