🇫🇷 Lesson 4: Les Connecteurs Logiques à C1

B2 lesson 21 gave you an argument's skeleton: d’abord, ensuite, enfin, certes… mais, en conclusion. That skeleton works. The trouble is that it announces itself — a French reader can hear the scaffolding, and at C1 the scaffolding is supposed to disappear into the joints.

This lesson is the full inventory of the connectives that do that: the pivot, the concession, the reinforcement, the reformulation and the close. Learn them with their register, because half of them are unusable in conversation.

🔀 Or — the Pivot, and the Most Useful Word Here

Or is not or. It introduces the fact that disturbs what was just said. It is the hinge on which French argument turns, it is one syllable long, and almost no learner uses it.

Le gouvernement annonce une baisse du chômage. Or, les chiffres publiés hier disent le contraire.
Il prétend n’avoir rien su. Or il présidait la réunion.

English has no single word for it. Now, yet and but in fact are all approximations. Whenever you are about to write mais en fait, try or instead.

💡 The move that sounds most C1

Certes, le coût est élevé. Il n’en demeure pas moins que l’inaction coûterait davantage.

You have conceded, and then restated your thesis without repeating it. That two-step is worth drilling until it is automatic — it is what DALF examiners are listening for.

Encore que deserves a note: it comes after the clause it qualifies, as a spoken-or-written afterthought. Je suis d’accord. Encore qu’il faudrait vérifier les chiffres.

🎚️ The Register Dial

Every connective sits somewhere on the ladder of B2 lesson 16. Mixing rungs is the commonest way an otherwise good text goes wrong.

⚠️ Two traps

Don't over-connect. A French paragraph does not need a connective in every sentence; juxtaposition and the colon carry logic too. Three connectives in five sentences reads as an essay written to a checklist.

Au final is not en définitive. It is a recent spoken form that grates in writing. Examiners notice it; so do employers.

🇫🇷 France

The concede-then-rebut pair is drilled into French students from the lycée onwards, because the dissertation's dialectical plan is built on it. Or is alive and frequent in speech as well as writing. The current spoken filler of choice is du coup, whose spread since the 2000s is a standing complaint in language columns — it has largely displaced donc in casual Parisian speech.

🇨🇦 Canada

Written Québécois uses the same inventory. Two spoken differences stand out: fait que (from ça fait que) is the everyday consequence marker where France now says du coup, and par contre is used very freely for contrast — including in contexts where a French purist would insist on en revanche. Québec's plain-language tradition also makes heavy soutenu connectives rarer in administrative text than in France.

✍️ Practice

Exercise 1 — Choose the connective

From: or · en revanche · d’autant plus que · autrement dit · faute de quoi.

  1. Il dit n’avoir rien signé. _____, sa signature figure au bas du contrat. (Answer: or)
  2. Le dossier doit partir lundi, _____ nous perdrons le marché. (Answer: faute de quoi)
  3. Paul adore le théâtre ; son frère, _____, n’y va jamais. (Answer: en revanche)
  4. Le projet est risqué, _____ nous n’avons aucune garantie. (Answer: d’autant plus que|d'autant plus que)
  5. Les recettes baissent et les coûts montent : _____, nous perdons de l’argent. (Answer: autrement dit)

Exercise 2 — Complete the concession

  • _____, le coût est élevé ; mais l’inaction coûterait plus cher. (Answer: certes)
  • Il n’en _____ pas moins que le problème reste entier. (Answer: demeure)
  • _____ est de constater que rien n’a changé. (Answer: force)
  • Je suis d’accord, _____ qu’il faudrait vérifier les chiffres. (Answer: encore)
  • Nous irons, _____ à partir plus tôt. (Answer: quitte)

Exercise 3 — Move up the register ladder

Replace the familiar connective with its soutenu equivalent.

  1. du coup → _____ (+ inversion) (Answer: aussi)
  2. en plus → qui plus _____ (Answer: est)
  3. au final → en _____ (Answer: définitive|definitive)
  4. sauf que → à ceci _____ que (Answer: près|pres)

Exercise 4 — Reformulate or add?

Write reformulation or ajout for each connective.

  • à savoir → _____ (Answer: reformulation)
  • sans compter que → _____ (Answer: ajout)
  • en d’autres termes → _____ (Answer: reformulation)
  • qui plus est → _____ (Answer: ajout)
