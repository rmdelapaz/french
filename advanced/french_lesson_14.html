🇫🇷 Lesson 14: L’Histoire des Mots

Welcome to C2. The grammar is finished; from here on the work is depth, precision and register. This lesson is the one that changes how you read a dictionary.

Consider eau and aquatique. Same meaning, no shared letters. Or doigt and digital. Or frêle and fragile, hôtel and hôpital, écouter and auscultation. In each pair, both words descend from the same Latin word — one by a thousand years of sound change in the mouths of speakers, the other lifted straight off the page by medieval scholars. French is a language with two layers, and once you can see the seam you can predict thousands of words.

💡 The Frankish fingerprint

Words beginning with gu- often betray a Germanic origin, because Frankish w- was borrowed as gu-: guerre (cf. English war), gagner, garder (ward), guêpe, Guillaume (William). Spot the pattern and you have identified a whole stratum of the language.

🔀 Les Doublets: the Central Mechanism

A doublet is a pair of French words from one Latin source: the mot populaire, worn smooth by centuries of speech, and the mot savant, copied from the written form. The division of labour is consistent — popular = concrete and everyday, learned = abstract and technical.

✍️ Why French Spelling Looks Like That

The silent letters are not decoration. They are etymological signposts, often reinserted deliberately by Renaissance printers who wanted the Latin visible.

Silent letterWordWhat it points to
silent ptempsLatin tempus → temporel
silent gdoigtdigitus → digital
silent dpoidspondus → pondéral
silent stemps, corpsLatin stem
circumflexhôpital, île, âmea lost s: hospitale, insula, anima
silent hthéâtre, rhétoriqueGreek spelling

The circumflex is the most useful of these: it usually marks a vanished s, which is why hôpital has one and hospitalier does not, and why fête relates to festival, île to insulaire, and forêt to forestier.

🌟 If you want to go further

Look up any word in the CNRTL's Trésor de la langue française online and read only the Étymol. et Hist. section at the bottom. It gives the first attested date, the source language and the semantic shifts. Ten minutes a day there will teach you more French vocabulary than any list, because you stop learning words and start learning families.

🇫🇷 France

France's terminology commissions coin learned replacements for English borrowings using exactly these Greek and Latin resources — logiciel, ordinateur, numérique, baladodiffusion. Some succeed completely (ordinateur has no rival), some fail entirely (mercatique for marketing). The pattern of which ones stick is a good indicator of how little institutional decree can do against usage — see lesson 23.

🇨🇦 Canada

Québec coins far more aggressively than France, and its coinages often travel back: courriel, clavardage, pourriel, baladodiffusion were all Québécois before they were French. The reason is structural — with English on every side, borrowing is not neutral, so the OQLF builds French words from the same classical stock instead. Québécois French also preserves some older French words that France lost (barrer la porte, char for a car), which are archaisms rather than anglicisms.
