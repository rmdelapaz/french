_Written 2026-09-23 before the advanced tier was built. Read this, plus

before touching `/advanced/`._

- **Live URL:** https://rays-french.netlify.app/advanced/

- **Beginner:** repo root (`/`) — 28 lessons (17 topic + 11 grammar), complete A1–A2.

## 1. Starting point — and why it constrains this tier more than its siblings

This course arrives at C1 with an unusually well-stocked cupboard. The beginner tier spent
**eleven** lessons on grammar, and the intermediate tier ran to **twenty-six** rather than the
siblings' twenty-four. Between them they have already retired a large slice of what most
"C1 French" syllabi open with.

**Closed by B1–B2 — may be recycled, never re-taught as new material:**

the entire subjunctive trigger inventory including the *subjonctif passé* and an explicit
mood-choice procedure (B1 L5–7) · all three si-clause types and mixed types (L4) · the compound
tenses — plus-que-parfait, futur antérieur, conditionnel présent + passé (L1–3) · *dont* /
*lequel* / *auquel* / *duquel* and *ce qui / ce que / ce dont* (L8) · verb + *à* / *de* /
bare infinitive (L9) · past-participle agreement with *avoir* (L10) · the passive, *on*,
*se faire* (L11) · the *-ant* trio (L12) · *discours indirect* and *concordance des temps* (L13) ·
the *faire causatif* (L14) · negation and restriction in depth (L15) · the **register ladder**
soutenu/courant/familier (L16) · **argot and verlan** (L17) · the **passé simple**, passé antérieur
and *subjonctif imparfait* for recognition (L18) · prefixes, suffixes, nominalisation basics and
*faux amis* (L19) · **idioms and expressions figées** (L20) · argumentation and *certes… mais* (L21) ·
formal letters and professional email (L22) · CV and *lettre de motivation* (L23) · authentic media
comprehension (L24) · a full **Québécois deep dive** (L25) · DELF B1/B2 strategy (L26).

That list matters because it removes, outright, six lessons that the Spanish advanced tier had to
teach: the subjunctive beyond the basics, register, slang, idiom/phraseology, formal correspondence
and media. **Every lesson below that touches one of them starts where B2 stopped**, and the
Québécois lesson in particular is *not* repeated — the francophonie lessons here deliberately cover
everywhere else.

## 2. What C1/C2 still owes a French learner

Audited against the CEFR C1/C2 descriptors, the DALF C1 and C2 specifications, and the B1–B2
syllabus above.

**A. Mood and syntax past the trigger list.** B2 taught *when* the subjunctive is required. C1 needs
the cases where it is a **choice** that changes meaning — the relative clause (*je cherche quelqu'un
qui sache*), the concessive paradigm (*quoi que*, *quel que*, *où que*, *si… que*), *le fait que*,
*ne fût-ce que* — plus the literary past subjunctives as *production* in formal writing, not just
recognition. And nothing yet teaches the **long sentence**: stacked subordination, *mise en relief*
(*c'est… qui/que*, *ce que… c'est*), dislocation, and inversion after *aussi* / *peut-être* /
*à peine*, which is how written French actually distributes emphasis.

**B. The nominal habit.** French prefers a noun where English prefers a verb (*dès la mise en œuvre
du dispositif*). B2's L19 touched nominalisation as word-formation; C1 needs it as a **style**, the
single most visible difference between a competent B2 text and a C1 one.

**C. Things French has that Spanish does not, and that therefore make this tier bigger:**

1. **Liaison, *enchaînement* and the *e caduc*.** A real phonological system with obligatory,
   forbidden and optional cases, *h aspiré*, the *loi des trois consonnes*, rhythm groups and the
   *accent d'insistance*. The Spanish advanced tier has no phonology lesson because Spanish
   orthography is near-transparent. French C1 listening and reading-aloud both stall without this.
2. **A written/spoken grammar split far wider than Spanish's.** Dislocation, in-situ questions
   (*tu vas où ?*), systematic *ne*-dropping, *y'a / chuis / t'as*, and a particle inventory
   (*quoi, ben, hein, du coup, genre*). B2's register lesson named the ladder; this teaches the
   **grammar** of its bottom rung.
3. **Les accords difficiles.** *Tout*, *même*, *quelque* vs *quel que*, *tel*, *leur*, *demi/nu/mi*,
   *ci-joint*, the numerals, colour adjectives, and *fait / laissé / dû* in compound tenses — a
   genuine system, tested in every *dictée* and every *concours*, with no Spanish counterpart of
   comparable size.
4. **French versification.** Syllable counting *with the e caduc*, the alexandrin and its *césure*,
   *enjambement*, the rhyme taxonomy. This is a discipline, not a footnote.
5. **The French academic genres.** The *dissertation* with its *plan dialectique*, the *commentaire
   composé*, the *explication de texte*, the *problématique*, and the ritual of the introduction —
   school genres with no direct Spanish equivalent, and the DALF C1 tests a fourth,
   the **synthèse de documents**, which has to be taught as a method.

**D. La francophonie beyond Québec.** French is pluricentric like Spanish, but its second centre is
already spent: Québec had a whole B2 lesson. What remains is (i) **European French outside France** —
Belgium, Switzerland, Luxembourg, the Aosta Valley, plus the French of the EU institutions, with
*septante/nonante*, belgicismes and helvétismes; and (ii) **African, Maghrebi and creole French** —
where the majority of the world's French speakers now live, with *nouchi*, *camfranglais*, the
Maghreb's diglossia, Haitian and Antillean creoles, Acadian and Louisiana French. Those are two
lessons of new material, not one.

**E. C2 registers as production, not comprehension.** B2 taught *reading* press and literature and
*writing* a formal letter. C2 owes: the legal and administrative register, business French
(meeting, negotiation, *compte rendu*, *rapport*), rhetoric and public speech including *la langue de
bois*, literary prose as stylistic analysis, poetry, and **translation** — which is itself a C2
competence with its own traps in the FR↔EN pair.

**F. The language's own politics.** The Académie, the *loi Toubon*, *écriture inclusive* and the
feminisation of job titles, the *franglais* argument, the OQLF, and the African claim that French
belongs to nobody. A C2 speaker is expected to hold a position in these debates, in French.

**G. Word history.** B2 decoded words from prefixes and suffixes. C2 decodes them from their
**layers** — the Latin *doublets savants et populaires* (*hôpital/hôtel*, *fragile/frêle*), the
Frankish substrate, the Greek learned stock, and the Arabic, Italian and English borrowings.

## 3. Lesson count — the derivation

Per [[course-size-sop]] the count is derived, not chosen. The siblings sit at 22 (Spanish advanced)
and 18 (Tagalog advanced); this tier lands at **24**, and that number was not the target.

Naive tally of every candidate topic in §2: **29**.

| Pressure | Effect |
|---|---|
| B1–B2 already closed the subjunctive inventory, register, argot, idiom/phraseology, formal correspondence, media comprehension **and** the Québécois deep dive | **−6** vs a standard C1/C2 French syllabus |
| Liaison / enchaînement / *e caduc* / prosody — no Spanish counterpart (§2C1) | **+1** |
| The grammar of spoken French, as grammar (§2C2) | **+1** |
| *Les accords difficiles* as a system (§2C3) | **+1** |
| Orthographic reform, punctuation and the French typographic code | **+1** |
| Francophonie needs **two** lessons because Québec is already spent (§2D) | **+2** |
| The French academic genres — dissertation, commentaire, explication (§2C5) | **+1** |
| The DALF's *synthèse de documents* is a taught method, so the exam lesson cannot be folded away | **+1** |
| French versification (§2C4) | **+1** |
| Humour, *jeux de mots* and *second degré* as a cultural competence | **+1** |
| Translation FR↔EN as its own craft | **+1** |

29 candidates, **five honest merges**, → **24**:

1. *Homophones grammaticaux* (*quoique/quoi que*, *quelque/quel que*, *plus tôt/plutôt*) fold into
   **Les Accords Difficiles** — each one is the same decision, "which agreement?", so a separate
   lesson would teach the same reflex twice.
2. *La litote* and hedging fold into **Atténuation, Implicite & Politesse**; irony and *second degré*
   go to the humour lesson. One "implicitness" lesson split by purpose, not two overlapping ones.
3. Press style and *la langue de bois* fold into **Rhétorique & Discours Public** — the same analytic
   skill — rather than a second media lesson after B2's L24.
4. Collocations and phraseology fold into **Régime Prépositionnel & Collocations**, because B2's L20
   already owns idioms and what is left is the *grammar* of fixed combination.
5. The self-directed maintenance plan folds into the **DALF capstone**, where it belongs as "what
   after the exam".

**Why not 22** (the Spanish count). Reaching 22 would mean cutting two of: the second francophonie
lesson, the phonology lesson, versification, or the academic-genres lesson. The first would put
three continents in one lesson; the second and third are the two places where French is measurably
harder than Spanish; the fourth is what the DALF C1 written paper is actually made of.
**Why not more.** Everything above 24 merged without loss, and each merge listed is a single arc
rather than a bundle.

Split: **C1 = 13 (1–13), C2 = 11 (14–24).** C1 is the longer half deliberately — it is where the
grammar and sociolinguistics live; C2 is register work on top of a finished grammar.

## 5. Architecture (mirrors `/intermediate/`, which mirrors `~/projects/spanish/advanced`)

- Pages are **numbered**: `/advanced/french_lesson_N.html`, N = 1…24.
- **Shared from the root — do NOT copy or fork:** `/learn.js`, `/glossary.js`, `/site-nav.js`,
  `/audio.js`, `/styles/main.css`, `/styles/learn.css`, `/favicon.png`. `learn.js` and `glossary.js`
  were **already** `/advanced/`-aware before this tier existed (`IS_ADV`, `TIER_BASE`, namespace
  `french-adv-`, SRS key `french-adv-srs`) — they needed no patch.
- Per-tier files that DO live here: `vocab-data.js`, `lesson-content.js`, `index.html`,
  `cando.js` + `cando.html`, `cheatsheet.js` + `cheatsheet.html`, `readings.js` + `readings.html`,
  `glossary.html` (loads the shared `/glossary.js`), `build_anki.py`.
- `cando.js` hardcodes its localStorage key — the advanced copy uses **`french-adv-cando`**.
- Companions are built by **splicing a new data block** (CANDO / SHEET / DIALOGUES) into a copy of
  the intermediate engine, never by rewriting the engine.
- Footer prev/next is **hand-written**. `add_footer_nav.py` at the root governs the beginner tier
  only; its `LESSONS` list is not touched.
- Script tags on an advanced lesson:
  `/site-nav.js`, `/audio.js`, `/advanced/vocab-data.js`, `/advanced/lesson-content.js`, `/learn.js`.
- L1's prev → `/intermediate/index.html`; L24's next → `cando.html`.
- `styles/main.css` gains `.tier-header.tier-c1/.tier-c2` and `.level-badge.level-c1/.level-c2`
  (violet / teal) — the only shared-file change this tier makes.

## 6. Conventions (inherited — follow exactly)

- **Never** hand-write "What You'll Learn" or "Key Takeaways" into a page. They come from
  `lesson-content.js` (`objectives` + `summary` + `time` + `level`), or the page renders two.
- Vocabulary tables must head their first two columns **`French`** and **`Pronunciation`** or
  `audio.js` will not attach the 🔊 buttons.
- Exercises: a `.practice-section` wrapping a `ul`/`ol`; each `<li>` carries `_____` (3+ underscores)
  **and** an inline `(Answer: x)`; **one blank per li**; accent-free alternatives after a `|`; the
  first alternative must be the fully accented canonical form, because `reveal()` fills that one.
  An answer may never contain `)` — the parser regex is `\(answer:\s*([^)]+)\)`.
- Reserve `___` for real blanks — use `…` in prose, or the QA regexes trip on it.
- Keep the 🇫🇷/🇨🇦 `.dialect-compare` box in every lesson. At C1/C2 it compares norms, institutions
  and register, not vocabulary.
- `vocab-data.js` entries are `{fr, pron, en}` with **literal** accents — never `\u{...}` escapes,
  because `build_anki.py` parses that file as JSON.
- **No vocabulary images at this tier**, for the same reason the intermediate tier has none: the
  vocabulary is grammatical and abstract metalanguage. Don't "fix" this later.
- `build_anki.py` here uses its **own** DECK_ID/MODEL_ID, distinct from both other decks.
- Pedagogy follows [[courseware-authoring-standard]]: objectives, growth-mindset framing, journal,
  summary, FAQ-style callouts, attributed footer — `© 2026 Ray de la Paz. French Advanced (C1–C2).`

## 7. Final state (built 2026-09-23)

**24 lessons** — C1 = 1–13, C2 = 14–24, exactly as planned in §4. Nothing was dropped or merged
during the build, so the §3 derivation still describes what shipped.

**Data & engine**
- `vocab-data.js` — 480 words across 24 lessons (`{fr, pron, en}`) + `titles` and `levels` maps,
  deliberately no `slugs` map and no `img` keys.
- `lesson-content.js` — 144 objectives (6 per lesson) and 144 summary points, plus per-lesson time
  and CEFR band. Nothing is hand-written into the pages.
- `/learn.js` and `/glossary.js` were **not touched** — both already detected `/advanced/`.
- `styles/main.css` gained four rules (`tier-c1`, `tier-c2`, `level-c1`, `level-c2`). That is the
  only shared-file edit this tier made.

**Companions:** `cando.html` (119 descriptors in 9 groups, key `french-adv-cando`) ·
`glossary.html` (480 rows, shared engine) · `cheatsheet.html` (19 categories, 166 rows) ·
`readings.html` (8 register pieces, 62 lines, 24 questions) · `french_advanced.apkg`
(574 notes / 1,148 cards, DECK/MODEL IDs 1758900042/41).

**Exercises:** every lesson carries exactly 4 `.practice-section` groups — 96 tier-wide,
438 checkable blanks, each group verified to score N/N on reveal-then-check.

**QA (headless Chromium over a local `python3 -m http.server`):** see `../../status.md` for the
full list. All 29 advanced pages and the sibling tiers: 0 console errors, 0 broken links across
206 targets, correct prev/next chain and `<h1>` numbering, no answer-key leaks, light + dark and
390px clean with 0px overflow, and the three localStorage namespaces verified isolated.

**Gotchas found during this build:**
- `learn.js` converts `_____` into an input only inside `.practice-box` / `.practice-section`.
  A `_____` in a **readings** question stem therefore renders literally — use `…` there. (The
  equivalent beginner/intermediate gotcha was about QA regexes; here it is visible to the learner.)
- The right way to build a companion is to copy the **intermediate** engine and replace only its
  data block (`CANDO` / `SHEET` / `DIALOGUES`), then change the localStorage key and the Anki
  DECK/MODEL IDs. Rewriting the engine is never necessary.
- `build_anki.py` reads `cheatsheet.js` through `node`, so the `SHEET` array must stay a pure data
  literal with no function calls in it.
