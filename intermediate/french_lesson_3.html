🇫🇷 Lesson 3: Le Conditionnel Présent & Passé

You have been using the conditional since A1 without being told: je voudrais un café, pourriez-vous m'aider ?. Those were memorised politeness formulas. Now you get the whole tense — its forms, and the four jobs it does in real French, including one that will change how you read the news.

🔧 Building the Conditionnel Présent

This is the easiest tense in French to build, because it recycles two things you already own: the stem of the futur simple and the endings of the imparfait.

💡 The formula

futur stem + imparfait endings (-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient)
Because the stem is the futur stem, every futur irregularity carries over untouched. If you know j'irai, you know j'irais. There is no second list to learn.

👂 Futur or conditionnel?

In the je form the difference is a single vowel: je parlerai (futur, closed "ay") vs je parlerais (conditionnel, open "eh"). Many native speakers barely distinguish them in fast speech, so context does the work. In writing, the difference is decisive — get it right.

🔧 Building the Conditionnel Passé

The fourth compound tense, built like all the others: the conditionnel of avoir or être plus the past participle. It means "would have done" — the thing that did not happen.

🎩 Use 1: Politeness — Now You Know Why

Je voudrais is simply the conditional of vouloir. Seeing it as a tense rather than a phrase unlocks a whole register of softened requests.

Blunt (présent)Polite (conditionnel)
Je veux parler au directeur.Je voudrais parler au directeur.
Vous pouvez m'aider ?Pourriez-vous m'aider ?
Ça vous dérange ?Cela vous dérangerait-il ?
J'ai une question.J'aurais une question.

💭 Use 2: Hypothesis

The conditional is the tense of the imagined. It is the main clause of a type-2 or type-3 si-sentence, which is Lesson 4's whole subject — but it also stands alone whenever the hypothesis is understood.

À ta place, je refuserais. — If I were you, I'd refuse.
Ce serait formidable ! — That would be wonderful!
Au cas où il pleuvrait, prends un parapluie. — In case it rains, take an umbrella.

Note au cas où: unlike most "in case" expressions in French, it takes the conditional, not the subjunctive. It is a useful exception to file away now, before the subjunctive arrives in Lesson 5.

🫵 Use 3: Advice and Reproach

Two verbs do most of this work. Devoir in the conditional gives advice; in the conditionnel passé it becomes a reproach. Pouvoir follows the same arc from suggestion to recrimination.

FormFrenchMeaning

devoir, conditionnelTu devrais partir.You should leave. (advice)
devoir, cond. passéTu aurais dû partir.You should have left. (reproach)
pouvoir, conditionnelOn pourrait sortir.We could go out. (suggestion)
pouvoir, cond. passéTu aurais pu me le dire !You could have told me!
impersonalIl vaudrait mieux attendre.It would be better to wait.

📰 Use 4: The Journalistic Conditional

This one has no English equivalent and it changes how you read French news. When a journalist reports something not yet verified, the verb goes into the conditional. It is not a hypothesis — it is a disclaimer built into the grammar.

FrenchPronunciationWhat it actually claims
Il y aurait des blessés.eel ee o-ray day blay-SAYThere are reportedly injuries. (unconfirmed)
Le suspect aurait fui.luh sü-spay o-ray FWEEThe suspect is said to have fled.
Selon la police, il s'agirait d'un accident.suh-lohn lah po-lees eel sah-zhee-ray duhn ak-see-DAHNPolice believe it was an accident.
Le ministre démissionnerait.luh mee-neestr day-mee-syo-nuh-RAYThe minister is reportedly resigning.

Read Le président aurait menti as "the president allegedly lied", never "the president would have lied". Miss the conditional and you will read rumour as fact — which is exactly what the tense exists to prevent.

⚠️ Éventuellement is not "eventually"

It means possibly, and it lives naturally alongside the conditional: Je pourrais éventuellement passer = "I could possibly drop by", not "I could eventually drop by". For "eventually", use finalement or à terme.

🇫🇷 France

The journalistic conditional is everywhere in French media — Le Monde, France Info, the lot. In formal speech you will also hear the very polite Auriez-vous l'obligeance de… and Je vous serais reconnaissant de…, both conditionals, both doing the work of a whole English paragraph of hedging.

🇨🇦 Canada

Radio-Canada uses the same journalistic conditional, so the reading skill transfers directly. In speech, Québécois often prefers j'aimerais where France reaches for je voudrais, and you will hear ça serait-tu possible ? — that -tu is the Québécois question particle, not the pronoun tu. It attaches to the verb and simply marks a yes/no question.

✍️ Practice

Exercise 1 — Form the conditionnel présent

  1. Je _____ un café, s'il vous plaît. (vouloir) (Answer: voudrais)
  2. Nous _____ partir plus tôt. (pouvoir) (Answer: pourrions)
  3. Tu _____ lui téléphoner. (devoir) (Answer: devrais)
  4. Elle _____ très contente. (être) (Answer: serait)
  5. Ils _____ volontiers. (venir) (Answer: viendraient)
  6. Vous _____ plus de temps. (avoir) (Answer: auriez)

Exercise 2 — Regret and reproach (conditionnel passé)

Fill in the auxiliary in the conditional.

  • Tu _____ dû me prévenir ! (Answer: aurais)
  • J'_____ pu t'aider, si tu avais demandé. (Answer: aurais)
  • Nous _____ aimé venir. (Answer: aurions)
  • Elle _____ partie sans nous. (Answer: serait)

Exercise 3 — Soften it

Rewrite the blunt verb in the conditional.

  1. Je veux réserver une table. → Je _____ réserver une table. (Answer: voudrais)
  2. Vous pouvez répéter ? → _____-vous répéter ? (Answer: Pourriez|pourriez)
  3. Il vaut mieux attendre. → Il _____ mieux attendre. (Answer: vaudrait)
  4. Ça te dérange ? → Ça te _____ ? (Answer: dérangerait|derangerait)

Exercise 4 — Read the news correctly

Write fait if the sentence states a confirmed fact, or rumeur if the conditional marks it as unverified.

  • Le ministre a démissionné ce matin. → _____ (Answer: fait)
  • Le ministre aurait démissionné ce matin. → _____ (Answer: rumeur)
  • Il y aurait une centaine de manifestants. → _____ (Answer: rumeur)
  • La police a confirmé l'arrestation. → _____ (Answer: fait)
