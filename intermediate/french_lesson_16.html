🇫🇷 Lesson 16: The Register Ladder

Until now, "correct French" has been a single target. At B2 it stops being one. The same thought can be expressed at three different levels of formality, and choosing the wrong one is a mistake even when every word is grammatical. Saying Qu'attendez-vous ? to a friend is as odd as saying Tu veux quoi ? to a judge.

🪜 The Three Rungs

RegisterWhere it livesFeel
soutenuliterature, speeches, formal letters, lawelevated, written, deliberate

There is a fourth rung below — vulgaire — which you should recognise and leave alone. And the crucial insight of this lesson: register is grammar, not just vocabulary. Swapping a word is only the surface.

❓ One Question, Three Registers

The clearest demonstration. Same meaning, three grammars.

FrenchPronunciationRegister

You learned all three question forms at A1. What you now know is that they are not interchangeable stylistic options — they are three social registers.

💡 On is the single best register tell

In speech, on has almost completely replaced nous as "we": On y va ?, On a mangé. Using nous in casual conversation sounds bookish. But in formal writing, nous is expected and on reads as vague. Flip between them deliberately and you sound native in both rooms.

🎩 Producing Soutenu

You need this for DELF written papers, formal letters and anything official. A handful of structures do most of the work.

🗣️ Recognising Familier

You do not have to produce this, but you must understand it — it is most of what you will hear.

FeatureExampleStandard form
Dropped neJ'sais pas.Je ne sais pas.
Reduced pronounsT'as vu ?Tu as vu ?
Truncationun resto, un ado, l'apremrestaurant, adolescent, après-midi
DislocationMoi, le fromage, j'adore ça.J'adore le fromage.
Question by intonationTu viens ?Est-ce que tu viens ?
Filler intensifiersC'est grave bien.C'est très bien.

Dislocation deserves a note: shifting a topic to the front or back with a resumptive pronoun (Moi, je…, Il est sympa, Paul) is extremely characteristic of spoken French and not an error. It is how speech organises information.

🤝 Tu or vous at B2

The A1 rule — vous for strangers, tu for friends — is true but too blunt for real situations. The B2 refinements:

SituationUsual choice
Colleagues of similar age, startup or creative sectortu quickly, often from day one
Colleagues in a bank, ministry, law firmvous, sometimes for years
Someone clearly older, or in a position of authorityvous until invited
Online forums, gaming, social mediatu by default
Service staff and customersvous both ways

The transition is explicit and worth knowing: On peut se tutoyer ? The move from vous to tu is offered by the older or senior person, and once made it is not reversed.

⚠️ Mixing registers is the giveaway

The most common advanced-learner error is not using the wrong register — it is using two at once: « Je ne sais point ce que tu fous. » A literary negative next to a crude verb. Pick a level and hold it for the whole utterance.

🇫🇷 France

France has a sharp register ladder and a strong culture of policing it — the Académie française, spelling certifications, and a genuine social penalty for getting it wrong in writing. Inversion questions survive in speech more than learners expect, especially short ones: Puis-je ?, Que sais-je ?, Comment allez-vous ?

🇨🇦 Canada

Québécois society tutoies far more readily than France — shop staff, colleagues and even some officials may use tu with you immediately, and it is friendly rather than rude. The register gap is wider, though: formal written Québécois is almost identical to French French, while spoken joual is very far from it. One local marker: the interrogative particle -tu, as in Tu viens-tu ? or C'est-tu loin ? — a yes/no question marker, nothing to do with the pronoun.

✍️ Practice

Exercise 1 — Name the register

Write soutenu, courant or familier.

  1. Que faites-vous ce soir ? → _____ (Answer: soutenu)
  2. Tu fais quoi ce soir ? → _____ (Answer: familier)
  3. Qu'est-ce que tu fais ce soir ? → _____ (Answer: courant)
  4. Il convient de signaler que… → _____ (Answer: soutenu)
  5. J'ai pas de fric. → _____ (Answer: familier)

Exercise 2 — Move up a rung

Give the courant equivalent of each familier word.

  • une bagnole → une _____ (Answer: voiture)
  • un boulot → un _____ (Answer: travail)
  • du fric → de l'_____ (Answer: argent)
  • bouffer → _____ (Answer: manger)
  • un resto → un _____ (Answer: restaurant)

Exercise 3 — Restore the standard form

  1. J'sais pas. → Je _____ sais pas. (Answer: ne|n')
  2. T'as vu ? → _____ as vu ? (Answer: Tu|tu)
  3. Y'a personne. → Il _____ a personne. (Answer: n'y|ny)
  4. C'est pas grave. → Ce n'est _____ grave. (Answer: pas)

Exercise 4 — Nous or on?

Write nous for a formal written context, on for casual speech.

  • A report to a ministry: "_____ avons constaté que…" (Answer: Nous|nous)
  • Texting a friend about dinner: "_____ y va ?" (Answer: On|on)
  • A thesis introduction: "_____ étudierons ici…" (Answer: Nous|nous)
  • Chatting after a film: "_____ a bien aimé." (Answer: On|on)
