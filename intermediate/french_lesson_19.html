🇫🇷 Lesson 19: Building & Decoding Vocabulary

At B2 you stop learning vocabulary one word at a time. French is built from a small set of roots plus a very regular kit of prefixes and suffixes — and once you can read that kit, an unfamiliar word often decodes itself. This lesson gives you the kit, plus the traps: the words that look English and are not.

Two useful predictions. In- assimilates to the following consonant: im- before p, b, m; il- before l; ir- before r. And sur-/sous- pairs are reliable opposites, which doubles your yield: knowing sous-estimer gives you surestimer free.

⚠️ The -ment ambiguity

-ment does two jobs. On a noun it is masculine and means an action: le changement. On an adverb it means "-ly": rapidement. Tell them apart by what precedes: an article means noun, no article means adverb.

📐 Nominalisation

The engine of formal French. Nominalisation turns a verb into a noun, which compresses a whole clause into a phrase. It is what makes headlines terse and essays dense.

ClauseNominalised
Les prix ont augmenté.l'augmentation des prix
L'usine a fermé.la fermeture de l'usine
Le ministre est arrivé.l'arrivée du ministre
On a supprimé des postes.la suppression de postes
La loi a été votée.le vote de la loi

Write an essay with a couple of nominalisations and it immediately reads at a higher register. Overuse them and it reads like a government circular — which is a real risk, and a real stylistic criticism in French.

⚖️ Nuance Pairs

Two French words, one English word. These separate B1 from B2.

PairDifferenceExamples
savoir / connaîtrea fact or a skill / be acquainted withJe sais nager. · Je connais Paris.
amener / apporterbring a person / bring a thingAmène ton frère. · Apporte du pain.
emmener / emportertake a person away / take a thing awayJe t'emmène. · J'emporte mon sac.
an / annéecounted unit / the span as livedtrois ans · toute l'année
jour / journéethe date / the day's durationtrois jours · Bonne journée !
entendre / écouterperceive / pay attentionJ'entends un bruit. · J'écoute la radio.
voir / regarderperceive / look at deliberatelyJe vois la mer. · Je regarde un film.
visiter / rendre visite àa place / a personvisiter Paris · rendre visite à ma tante

The an/année family follows one logic across all four pairs (an/année, jour/journée, matin/matinée, soir/soirée): the short form counts, the long form describes the experience of the time passing.

🇫🇷 France

France borrows English words freely in speech — le week-end, un mail, le shopping, un job — even where the Académie has proposed a French alternative. Nominalisation is prized in formal writing and mocked as langue de bois when politicians overdo it.

🇨🇦 Canada

Québec coins French replacements far more actively, and they stick: un courriel (email), clavardage (chat), baladodiffusion (podcasting), magasinage (shopping), stationnement (parking). The paradox is that spoken Québécois carries plenty of English structural calques (prendre une marche, c'est le fun) while its formal vocabulary is more purist than France's. Knowing both sets of words is a real B2 advantage.

Exercise 1 — Decode the prefix

What does the prefix add? Write the meaning in English.

  1. refaire = to do _____ (Answer: again)
  2. défaire = to _____ (Answer: undo)
  3. surestimer = to _____ estimate (Answer: over)
  4. sous-payé = _____ paid (Answer: under)
  5. mécontent = _____ (Answer: discontent|unhappy|displeased)

Exercise 2 — Gender from the suffix

Exercise 3 — Nominalise

  1. Les prix ont augmenté. → l'_____ des prix (Answer: augmentation)

  3. Le ministre est arrivé. → l'_____ du ministre (Answer: arrivée|arrivee)
  4. On a supprimé des postes. → la _____ de postes (Answer: suppression)

Exercise 4 — Faux amis and nuance pairs

  • I'm currently working. → Je travaille _____. (Answer: actuellement)
  • I know how to swim. → Je _____ nager. (Answer: sais)
  • I know Paris. → Je _____ Paris. (Answer: connais)
  • Bring your brother. → _____ ton frère. (Answer: Amène|amène|Amene|amene)
  • Bring some bread. → _____ du pain. (Answer: Apporte|apporte)
