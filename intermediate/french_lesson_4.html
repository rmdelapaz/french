🇫🇷 Lesson 4: Les Phrases avec Si — The Three Hypotheses

You now own all four tenses the French hypothesis system needs: présent, imparfait, plus-que-parfait and conditionnel. This lesson wires them together. There are exactly three patterns, and once you have them, you can talk about anything from a plan for tonight to a regret from ten years ago.

🧭 The Three Patterns at a Glance

TypeSi clauseMain clauseMeaning
1 — realsi + présentfuturIt might well happen
2 — unreal presentsi + imparfaitconditionnel présentIt isn't so, but imagine
3 — unreal pastsi + plus-que-parfaitconditionnel passéIt didn't happen; too late

🚫 The rule that breaks everyone

Never put a futur or a conditionnel after si meaning "if". Not si j'aurai, not si je voudrais. The future or conditional goes in the other clause. French keeps si clean: présent, imparfait, or plus-que-parfait — those three and nothing else.

1️⃣ Type 1 — The Real Possibility

Use it for anything genuinely on the table: plans, threats, promises, weather.

Si j'ai le temps, je viendrai. — If I have time, I'll come.
S'il pleut demain, on restera à la maison. — If it rains tomorrow, we'll stay home.

Two variations worth knowing. The main clause can be an imperative — Si tu as faim, mange ! — or a present for a general truth: Si on chauffe l'eau à 100 degrés, elle bout. And note the elision: si + il becomes s'il, but si elle never contracts.

2️⃣ Type 2 — The Unreal Present

The condition is false right now, or very unlikely. This is the daydream, the polite suggestion, the "if I were you".

Si j'avais le temps, je viendrais. — If I had time, I'd come. (but I don't)
Si j'étais riche, j'achèterais une maison en Provence.
Si j'étais toi, je refuserais. — If I were you, I'd refuse.

French has no separate "were" form: si j'étais is simply the imparfait, used for everyone. The shorter à ta place ("in your position") does the same job with no si at all, and sounds more natural in speech.

3️⃣ Type 3 — The Unreal Past

The door is closed. This is regret, blame, and the endless post-mortem of what might have been.

Si j'avais eu le temps, je serais venu. — If I'd had time, I would have come.
Si tu m'avais écouté, ça ne serait pas arrivé. — If you'd listened to me, that wouldn't have happened.

Strip off the main clause and you are back to the regret formula from Lesson 1: Si seulement j'avais su ! Same grammar, half the sentence.

🔀 Mixed Types

Real life does not respect the table. When a past condition has a present result, mix freely: keep the si clause in the plus-que-parfait and put the main clause in the conditionnel présent.

FrenchMeaning
Si j'avais étudié la médecine, je serais médecin aujourd'hui.If I had studied medicine, I'd be a doctor today.
Si elle n'avait pas raté son train, elle serait déjà là.If she hadn't missed her train, she'd already be here.
Si j'étais plus organisé, j'aurais fini hier.If I were more organised, I'd have finished yesterday.

❓ The Other Si: "Whether"

French uses the same little word for "if" and for "whether", and the tense rule applies to only one of them. After si meaning whether — in an indirect question — the futur and the conditionnel are perfectly legal.

SentenceWhich si?Legal?
Je me demande s'il viendra.whether (indirect question)✅ Futur allowed
Si j'aurai le temps, je viendrai.if (condition)❌ Never
Elle a demandé si je serais là.whether (reported question)✅ Conditionnel allowed

Quick test: if you can swap in "whether" in English, the tense rule does not apply.

🇫🇷 France

In casual French you will hear type 2 used where the textbook wants type 1 — Si tu venais, ce serait sympa instead of Si tu viens, ce sera sympa — because the imparfait softens an invitation. You will also hear the non-standard si j'aurais from native speakers; it is stigmatised, and every French child is corrected for it.

🇨🇦 Canada

Same three patterns. In informal Québécois, though, the type-3 si clause is very often flattened to a conditionnel passé on both sides: Si j'aurais su, j'aurais pas venu — instantly recognisable, heavily stigmatised in writing, and immortalised by the film La Guerre des tuques. Recognise it; do not reproduce it on an exam.

✍️ Practice

Exercise 1 — Type 1 (real)

Put the verb into the futur simple.

  1. Si j'ai le temps, je te _____. (téléphoner) (Answer: téléphonerai|telephonerai)
  2. S'il fait beau, nous _____ à la plage. (aller) (Answer: irons)
  3. Si tu travailles bien, tu _____ ton examen. (réussir) (Answer: réussiras|reussiras)

Exercise 2 — Type 2 (unreal present)

Put the verb into the conditionnel présent.

  1. Si j'avais de l'argent, j'_____ une voiture. (acheter) (Answer: achèterais|acheterais)
  2. Si nous étions libres, nous _____ avec vous. (venir) (Answer: viendrions)
  3. Si tu parlais chinois, tu _____ travailler à Shanghai. (pouvoir) (Answer: pourrais)
  4. Si j'étais toi, je _____ non. (dire) (Answer: dirais)

Exercise 3 — Type 3 (unreal past)

Fill in the missing auxiliary or verb form.

  • Si j'avais su, je ne _____ pas venu. (être, conditionnel) (Answer: serais)
  • Si tu m'avais prévenu, j'_____ pu t'aider. (avoir, conditionnel) (Answer: aurais)
  • Si nous _____ parti plus tôt, nous aurions eu le train. (être, plus-que-parfait) (Answer: étions|etions)
  • Si elle _____ eu le choix, elle serait restée. (avoir, plus-que-parfait) (Answer: avait)

Exercise 4 — Name the type

Write 1, 2 or 3.

  1. Si tu viens, on sera content. → type _____ (Answer: 1)
  2. Si j'avais eu le temps, je serais passé. → type _____ (Answer: 3)
  3. Si j'étais plus jeune, je voyagerais davantage. → type _____ (Answer: 2)
  4. Si vous partez maintenant, vous arriverez à l'heure. → type _____ (Answer: 1)
