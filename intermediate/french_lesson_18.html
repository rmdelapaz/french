🇫🇷 Lesson 18: Reading Literary & Journalistic French

Open any French novel, or a serious newspaper's long-form piece, and you will meet verb forms you have never been taught: il fut, elle vint, ils partirent. This is the passé simple — the written narrative past. Nobody speaks it. Everybody reads it. This lesson is about recognition, not production, and it is the lesson that opens authentic French literature to you.

📖 Why It Exists

French split its past tenses by register, not by meaning. Spoken French uses the passé composé for completed events. Written narrative uses the passé simple for exactly the same job.

Spoken / informal writingLiterary narrative
Il est arrivé à midi.Il arriva à midi.
Elle a ouvert la porte.Elle ouvrit la porte.
Ils sont partis sans rien dire.Ils partirent sans rien dire.

Crucially, the imparfait does not change. A literary text alternates passé simple (events) with imparfait (background) exactly as speech alternates passé composé with imparfait. The system you learned at A2 still works — one of its two halves just has a second costume.

💡 The two shapes to recognise

-a / -èrent → an -er verb. Il parla, ils allèrent, elle regarda.
-it / -irent → an -ir or -re verb. Il finit, ils sortirent, elle répondit.
Watch the trap: il finit is identical in the present and the passé simple. Only context separates them.

⏮️ Le Passé Antérieur

The literary partner of the plus-que-parfait: the auxiliary in the passé simple + past participle. It marks a past event completed immediately before another passé simple, and appears almost exclusively after dès que, quand, à peine and lorsque.

Dès qu'il eut fini, il se leva et sortit. — As soon as he had finished, he rose and went out.
À peine fut-elle partie que le téléphone sonna. — Hardly had she left when the phone rang.

In speech you would say Dès qu'il avait fini… or restructure entirely. Recognition only.

🎭 The Imperfect Subjunctive

The last piece of literary machinery. Where modern French uses the present subjunctive after a past main clause, very formal writing uses the imparfait du subjonctif. You will meet it in Flaubert, in legal texts, and in deliberately ornate prose.

LiteraryModern equivalent
Il voulait qu'elle vînt.Il voulait qu'elle vienne.
Bien qu'il fût tard…Bien qu'il soit tard…
Je craignais qu'il ne mourût.Je craignais qu'il ne meure.

The circumflex on the third-person singular (fût, vînt, eût) is the giveaway. Note eût (subjunctive) against eut (passé simple) — one accent, two tenses.

🇫🇷 France

The passé simple is compulsory school reading — every French child meets it through fairy tales (Il était une fois… le prince épousa la princesse) and then through the nineteenth-century novel. It survives in formal history writing and in children's storybooks, which makes graded readers a surprisingly good place to practise it.

🇨🇦 Canada

Identical usage: Québécois literature and Radio-Canada's written features use the passé simple exactly as French publications do. The difference is oral. Some older rural Québécois speech preserved passé simple forms far longer than France did, and you will occasionally meet them in transcribed folk narratives and in the dialogue of historical novels such as Maria Chapdelaine.

✍️ Practice

Exercise 1 — Identify the infinitive

  1. il fut → _____ (Answer: être|etre)
  2. il eut → _____ (Answer: avoir)
  3. il fit → _____ (Answer: faire)
  4. elle vint → _____ (Answer: venir)
  5. il prit → _____ (Answer: prendre)
  6. ils virent → _____ (Answer: voir)

Exercise 2 — Into the passé composé

Give the auxiliary + participle in the spoken equivalent.

  • il arriva → il _____ arrivé (Answer: est)
  • elle parla → elle _____ parlé (Answer: a)
  • ils partirent → ils _____ partis (Answer: sont)
  • il fit → il _____ fait (Answer: a)

Exercise 3 — Which tense is it?

Write passé simple, imparfait or passé antérieur.

  1. Il pleuvait ce soir-là. → _____ (Answer: imparfait)
  2. Soudain, il entra. → _____ (Answer: passé simple|passe simple)
  3. Dès qu'il eut fini, il sortit. The first verb is _____ (Answer: passé antérieur|passe anterieur)
  4. Elle portait une robe bleue. → _____ (Answer: imparfait)

Exercise 4 — Unpack the headline

Fill in the missing word of the full sentence.

  • Grève des transports à Paris → Il y a _____ grève des transports à Paris. (Answer: une)
  • Un incendie ravage l'usine (yesterday) → Un incendie _____ ravagé l'usine. (Answer: a)
  • Le ministre aurait démissionné → this is a _____ , not a confirmed fact. (Answer: rumeur)
