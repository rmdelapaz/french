🇫🇷 Lesson 12: Participe Présent, Gérondif & Adjectif Verbal

French has one ending, -ant, that does three completely different jobs. Confuse them and your sentences will wobble; separate them and you gain one of the most useful tools in written French — a way to pack two ideas into a single clause.

🔧 Building the Form

Take the nous form of the present tense, drop -ons, add -ant. This is the same stem that builds the imparfait, so you already know it.

Infinitivenous form-ant form
parlernous parlonsparlant
finirnous finissonsfinissant
prendrenous prenonsprenant
fairenous faisonsfaisant
boirenous buvonsbuvant
mangernous mangeonsmangeant

Exactly three verbs are irregular — and they are the three you would guess:

FrenchPronunciationMeaning
étantay-TAHNbeing (être)
ayantay-YAHNhaving (avoir)
sachantsah-SHAHNknowing (savoir)

1️⃣ Le Gérondif — en + -ant

Add en in front and you have the gérondif. It is invariable, and it has one iron rule: it must share the subject of the main verb.

It expresses three relationships, and context tells you which:

SenseFrenchMeaning
Simultaneity (while)Il chante en travaillant.He sings while working.
Means (by)On apprend en pratiquant.You learn by practising.
Condition (if)En partant maintenant, tu l'auras.If you leave now, you'll catch it.
Moment (as, on)En sortant, j'ai vu Paul.As I left, I saw Paul.

🚫 The subject rule — and the classic error

En sortant du cinéma, j'ai vu Paul means I came out of the cinema. If Paul was the one coming out, this sentence is simply wrong — you would need J'ai vu Paul qui sortait du cinéma. Because the gérondif is anchored to the main subject, it can never introduce a new one. English is much looser here; French is not.

And note what the gérondif cannot do: it never expresses purpose. "I went out to buy bread" is Je suis sorti pour acheter du pain, never en achetant.

➕ Tout en — Simultaneity or Concession

Adding tout either underlines that two things happen at once, or introduces a contradiction.

Il lit tout en écoutant de la musique. — He reads while listening to music. (emphatic simultaneity)
Tout en étant très riche, il vit simplement. — Although very rich, he lives simply. (concession)

2️⃣ Le Participe Présent — the Bare Form

Without en, the -ant form is a participle. It is invariable, it behaves like a verb (it can take objects and adverbs), and its main job is to replace a relative clause with qui — which makes writing tighter.

Relative clauseParticipe présent
les gens qui habitent iciles gens habitant ici
une loi qui interdit le tabacune loi interdisant le tabac
les étudiants qui parlent trois languesles étudiants parlant trois langues

It also expresses cause, usually at the head of a sentence — a register noticeably more formal than parce que:

Ne sachant pas quoi faire, j'ai appelé un ami. — Not knowing what to do, I called a friend.
Étant malade, il est resté chez lui. — Being ill, he stayed home.
Ayant fini son travail, elle est partie. — Having finished her work, she left. (the compound form: ayant/étant + participle)

You will also meet the fixed expression étant donné que ("given that"), which is simply this participle frozen into a conjunction.

3️⃣ L'Adjectif Verbal — the One That Agrees

Sometimes an -ant word has stopped being a verb and become a plain adjective. Then — and only then — it agrees in gender and number.

Participe présent (invariable)Adjectif verbal (agrees)
une histoire fatiguant les enfants
(a story tiring the children)		une histoire fatigante
(a tiring story)
des enfants obéissant à leurs parentsdes enfants obéissants
une équipe gagnant tous ses matchsune équipe gagnante

💡 The test

Can you put a direct object or an adverb after it, or negate it with ne… pas? Then it is still a verb → participle, invariable. Can you replace it with an ordinary adjective like intéressant or difficile? Then it is an adjective → it agrees.

✏️ The Spelling Pairs

A handful of words spell the two forms differently. These turn up in exams precisely because they are easy to miss.

The pattern is reliable: the verb form keeps the -gu- or -qu- of the stem, while the adjective simplifies to -g- or -c- and often ends in -ent.

🇫🇷 France

The bare participe présent is a marker of written French — administrative texts are full of les personnes souhaitant s'inscrire, toute demande parvenant après le…. In speech it is rare; people say les gens qui habitent ici. The gérondif, by contrast, is completely normal in conversation: J'ai appris ça en lisant le journal.

🇨🇦 Canada

Identical grammar and identical register split. Québécois speech uses the gérondif freely — En s'en allant, j'ai croisé Manon. One thing to listen for: être après + infinitive (Lesson 9) often does the work that a gérondif or en train de would do in France, so J'suis après travailler and Je travaille can cover ground a gérondif might otherwise take.

Fill in en + the right form.

  • Elle chante en _____ la vaisselle. (laver) (Answer: lavant)
  • En _____ tôt, tu éviteras les bouchons. (partir) (Answer: partant)
