🇫🇷 Lesson 21: Argumentation, Opinion & Debate

B2 is the level at which you stop describing and start arguing. The CEFR descriptor says you can "develop an argument giving reasons in support of or against a particular point of view" — and the DELF B2 tests exactly that, twice. This lesson gives you the moves and the language to make them.

⚠️ Negating an opinion flips the mood

Straight from Lesson 7: Je pense que c'est vrai but Je ne pense pas que ce soit vrai. In an argued essay you will negate opinion verbs constantly — so the subjunctive will appear whether you plan for it or not.

🏛️ The French Essay Plan

French schooling teaches one structure and examiners expect it. Learn it and half the marking scheme is satisfied before you write a sentence.

PartJobTypical opener
IntroductionSet the scene, state the problématique, announce the planDe nos jours, … On peut donc se demander si…
ThèseThe case forD'une part, … En effet, …
AntithèseThe case againstCependant, … En revanche, …
SynthèseResolve, take a positionIl semble donc que… Tout bien pesé, …
ConclusionAnswer the problématique, open outwardEn conclusion, … On peut se demander si…

The problématique is the heart of it: the essay's central question, stated explicitly at the end of the introduction. An essay without one reads to a French examiner as an essay without a point.

🤝 Concede, Then Rebut — the Core B2 Move

This is what separates a B2 argument from a B1 list of opinions. You grant your opponent's point, then explain why it does not settle the matter.

💡 En effet is not "in effect"

En effet confirms and explains what you just said — "indeed", "this is because". For the English "in effect", use en réalité or en fait. And note or (pronounced "or"), a small but powerful connector meaning "now, as it happens", used to introduce the fact that turns an argument.

🪶 Hedging — Claiming Only What You Mean

Overstating is a B2 error just as much as understating. These let you make a claim with calibrated confidence.

Several of these are conditionals (Lesson 3) doing register work — il semblerait, on pourrait. The conditional is the mood of the careful claim.

🇫🇷 France

The thèse/antithèse/synthèse plan is drilled from the lycée onwards and is expected in the baccalauréat, at university and in the DELF. French debate culture tolerates — even enjoys — direct disagreement, so Je ne suis pas du tout d'accord is normal rather than rude. What is judged harshly is an argument with no visible structure.

🇨🇦 Canada

The same written plan is taught in Québec's CÉGEPs. Spoken debate style is noticeably softer and more consensus-seeking than in France — hedges like mettons ("let's say"), j'dirais and admettons appear far more often, and blunt contradiction lands harder. If you are arguing in Montréal, lean on the concession moves above; in Paris, you can come straight to the point.

✍️ Practice

Exercise 1 — Choose the connector

  1. To introduce a contrast: en _____ (Answer: revanche)
  2. To add a further point: de _____ (Answer: plus)
  3. To draw a consequence: par _____ (Answer: conséquent|consequent)
  4. To explain what you just said: en _____ (Answer: effet)
  5. To close the essay: en _____ (Answer: conclusion)

Exercise 2 — Concede, then rebut

  • _____, c'est cher, mais ça vaut le coup. (admittedly) (Answer: Certes|certes)
  • Il est vrai que c'est risqué ; _____, il faut essayer. (however) (Answer: cependant|toutefois|néanmoins|neanmoins)
  • Il n'en _____ pas moins que le problème persiste. (Answer: reste)
  • Quoi qu'il en _____, la décision est prise. (Answer: soit)

Exercise 3 — Mood after an opinion verb

  1. Je pense que c'_____ vrai. (être) (Answer: est)
  2. Je ne pense pas que ce _____ vrai. (être) (Answer: soit)
  3. Il est indéniable que le climat _____. (changer) (Answer: change)
  4. Il est peu probable qu'il _____ raison. (avoir) (Answer: ait)

Exercise 4 — Name the essay part

Write introduction, thèse, antithèse or conclusion.

  • "On peut donc se demander si…" → _____ (Answer: introduction)
  • "D'une part, le télétravail réduit les trajets." → _____ (Answer: thèse|these)
  • "Cependant, l'isolement pèse lourd." → _____ (Answer: antithèse|antithese)
  • "En définitive, il apparaît que…" → _____ (Answer: conclusion)
