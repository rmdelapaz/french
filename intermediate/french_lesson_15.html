🇫🇷 Lesson 15: Negation & Restriction in Depth

At A1 you learned ne… pas and its family. That gets you negation. This lesson gets you precision: how to say "only", how to negate with the restraint of formal writing, how to stack negatives, and what is really going on when a French speaker drops the ne entirely.

🎯 Ne… que — "Only"

The most useful item in this lesson, and the one most often mistaken for a negation. Ne… que does not deny anything — it restricts.

FrenchPronunciationMeaning
Je n'ai que dix euros.zhuh nay kuh dee zuh-ROHI only have ten euros.
Il ne boit que de l'eau.eel nuh bwah kuh duh LOHHe only drinks water.
Elle n'arrive qu'à midi.el nah-reev kah mee-DEEShe doesn't arrive until noon.
Je ne fais que passer.zhuh nuh fay kuh pah-SAYI'm only passing through.

💡 The de-rule does not apply

A real negation turns the article into de: Je n'ai pas de pain. But ne… que is not a negation, so the article survives intact: Je n'ai que du pain. Getting this right is a strong B2 signal.

Where you put que changes the meaning

Que sits immediately before whatever is being restricted — so moving it moves the meaning.

FrenchWhat is restricted
Je ne bois que du thé le matin.only tea (nothing else to drink)
Je ne bois du thé que le matin.only in the morning (not later)
Seul Paul est venu.only Paul (and nobody else)

🎩 The Formal Negatives

These belong to careful writing and elevated speech. Recognise all of them; produce the first two.

FrenchPronunciationMeaning
ne… guèrenuh GAIRhardly, scarcely
ne… nullementnuh nül-MAHNnot at all, in no way
ne… aucunementnuh oh-kün-MAHNin no way whatsoever
ne… pointnuh PWANnot (literary/archaic)
nul ne / nul n'estnül nuhnobody (formal)

Il n'est guère surprenant que… — "It is hardly surprising that…" is a genuinely useful B2 opener for an argued essay.

➕ Stacking Negatives

Unlike English, French lets you combine negative words freely — and they reinforce rather than cancel each other. There is a fixed order.

FrenchMeaning
Je ne dis plus rien.I'm not saying anything any more.
Il n'y a plus jamais personne.There's never anyone any more.
Je n'ai plus rien à ajouter.I have nothing further to add.
On ne voit jamais personne ici.You never see anyone here.

The order is roughly plus → jamais → rien / personne. What you cannot do is combine two of these with pas: ne pas rien is wrong. Pas steps aside when another negative word arrives.

🔗 Ni… ni…

The negative of et. Note what happens to the articles: indefinite and partitive articles disappear; definite articles and possessives stay.

PositiveNegative
Je veux du thé et du café.Je ne veux ni thé ni café.
J'aime le thé et le café.Je n'aime ni le thé ni le café.
Il est riche et généreux.Il n'est ni riche ni généreux.
Paul et Marie sont venus.Ni Paul ni Marie ne sont venus.

Related and very common: non plus is the negative of aussi. — Je n'aime pas ça. — Moi non plus. Never moi aussi after a negative.

♾️ Negating an Infinitive

Here French is simpler than you would expect: both negative words sit together, in front of the infinitive.

FrenchMeaning
Je te demande de ne pas partir.I'm asking you not to leave.
Il préfère ne rien dire.He'd rather not say anything.
Essayer de ne plus fumer.To try not to smoke any more.
Ne pas se pencher au dehors.Do not lean out. (sign)

Compare the conjugated verb, where the two halves split around it: Je ne pars pas. With an infinitive they never split.

🚷 Sans — Negation Without Ne

Sans is already negative, so it never takes ne or pas, and it pulls the other negative words toward it.

sans rien dire (without saying anything) · sans jamais se plaindre (without ever complaining) · sans personne (without anyone) · sans aucun doute (without any doubt).

🗣️ The Disappearing Ne

In spoken French, the ne is dropped constantly. This is not sloppiness by a few speakers — it is the normal spoken register, used by everyone including the highly educated.

WrittenSpokenPronunciation
Je ne sais pas.J'sais pas.shay PAH
Ce n'est pas grave.C'est pas grave.say pah GRAHV
Il n'y a personne.Y'a personne.yah pair-SON
Je n'en veux plus.J'en veux plus.zhahn vuh PLÜ

⚠️ Understand it, don't write it

Dropping ne in a DELF written paper, an email or an essay costs you marks. Keeping it in casual conversation makes you sound like a textbook. The B2 skill is knowing which room you are in — which is exactly what Lesson 16 is about.

🇫🇷 France

Ne-dropping is near-universal in casual speech, but it returns in formal registers, on the radio and in lectures. Note the treacherous ambiguity of spoken plus: J'en veux plus can mean "I want more" (pronounced "plüss") or "I don't want any more" (pronounced "plü"). The final s carries the whole meaning.

🇨🇦 Canada

Ne-dropping is if anything even more complete in Québécois speech. The distinctive local negative is pantoute (from pas en tout), meaning "not at all": J'ai pas aimé ça pantoute. You will also hear rien que used very freely for "only" — Rien qu'à voir, on voit bien, a well-known Québécois saying meaning "you can tell at a glance".

✍️ Practice

Exercise 1 — Ne… que

Fill in the missing half of the restriction.

  1. Je n'ai _____ dix euros. (Answer: que|qu')
  2. Il ne boit _____ de l'eau. (Answer: que|qu')
  3. Elle n'arrive _____ à midi. (Answer: qu'|que)
  4. Je _____ fais que passer. (Answer: ne|n')

Exercise 2 — Negation or restriction?

Write négation or restriction.

  • Je n'ai pas de pain. → _____ (Answer: négation|negation)
  • Je n'ai que du pain. → _____ (Answer: restriction)
  • Il ne mange jamais de viande. → _____ (Answer: négation|negation)
  • Il ne mange que de la viande. → _____ (Answer: restriction)

Exercise 3 — Stack and combine

  1. I'm not saying anything any more. → Je ne dis _____ rien. (Answer: plus)
  2. You never see anyone here. → On ne voit jamais _____ ici. (Answer: personne)
  3. I want neither tea nor coffee. → Je ne veux _____ thé ni café. (Answer: ni)
  4. — Je n'aime pas ça. — Moi _____ plus. (Answer: non)

Exercise 4 — Negate the infinitive

Put both negative words together in front of the infinitive.

  • Je te demande de _____ partir. (ne + pas) (Answer: ne pas)
  • Il préfère _____ dire. (ne + rien) (Answer: ne rien)
  • Essaie de _____ fumer. (ne + plus) (Answer: ne plus)
  • Il est parti _____ rien dire. (no ne needed) (Answer: sans)
