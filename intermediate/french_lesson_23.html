🇫🇷 Lesson 23: Le CV & la Lettre de Motivation

A French CV is not an English one translated. It has its own sections, its own order, and — in France — its own expectations about what personal information belongs on it. The lettre de motivation that accompanies it has an even more fixed shape. This lesson gives you both, in the two conventions the course covers.

Order and conventions: one page unless you have fifteen years of experience; reverse chronological within each section; no full sentences — fragments and verbs do the work.

⚠️ Formation is not "formation"

It means education and training. And une expérience on a CV means a job or placement, not a feeling. Add these to your Lesson 19 faux-amis list.

Best practice is to give the CEFR band directly — Français : B2 (DELF) — which is unambiguous and verifiable. Overclaiming here is quickly exposed in the interview.

✉️ The Lettre de Motivation: Vous — Moi — Nous

The structure French recruiters expect, in three short paragraphs.

ParagraphFocusContent
Vousthe employerShow you understand their organisation and the role.
MoiyouTwo or three concrete pieces of evidence that you fit it.
NoustogetherWhat you'd bring, plus a request for an interview.

Starting with vous rather than je is the whole trick. A letter that opens Je suis étudiant et je cherche un stage has already failed; one that opens with what the company does has not.

Objet : candidature au poste de chargé de communication

Madame, Monsieur,

[vous] Votre agence s'est imposée ces dernières années comme une référence en communication culturelle, et le poste de chargé de communication que vous proposez correspond précisément au type de mission qui m'intéresse.

[moi] Titulaire d'un master en communication, j'ai assuré pendant deux ans le suivi éditorial d'un festival régional, où j'ai encadré une équipe de quatre personnes et mis en place une stratégie de contenus qui a doublé notre audience en ligne.

[nous] Je serais ravi de mettre cette expérience au service de vos projets et me tiens à votre disposition pour un entretien.

Je vous prie d'agréer, Madame, Monsieur, l'expression de mes salutations distinguées.

🇫🇷 vs 🇨🇦 — What Goes on the CV

This is the one place where the two conventions genuinely diverge, and getting it wrong is costly.

ItemFranceQuébec
PhotoStill common, though decliningNever — seen as inviting discrimination
Age / date of birthOften includedNever
Marital statusSometimes includedNever
NationalitySometimesOnly work authorisation, if relevant
LengthOne pageTwo pages is normal
References"disponibles sur demande"Often listed in full

If you are applying in Québec with a France-style CV, strip the photo, age and marital status first. Québec's employment-equity rules make those fields actively unwelcome.

Prétentions salariales is another faux ami — it means salary expectations, nothing to do with pretending. And always have a question ready for the last one; having none reads as lack of interest.

🇫🇷 France

The lettre de motivation remains genuinely important in France and is often read before the CV. Diplomas carry real weight — the institution you attended is named and noticed. Interviews tend to be formal, with vous throughout, and discussing salary too early is considered poor form.

🇨🇦 Canada

Québec leans North American: the CV does most of the work, the cover letter is shorter, and experience outranks the prestige of your school. Interviews are friendlier and may switch to tu quickly. Vocabulary differs too — you will see une job (feminine in Québec), un emploi d'été, le salaire horaire, and postuler sur un poste alongside France's postuler à.
