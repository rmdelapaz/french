# French B1/B2 — Audit & Build Roadmap

_Written 2026-09-22 before the intermediate tier was built. Read this (and `../../docs/a1-a2-roadmap.md`)
before touching `/intermediate/`._

## 1. Starting point

The beginner tier is **complete at A1/A2**: 28 lessons (17 topic + 11 "Grammar Essentials"), a
learning layer (`learn.js`), and five companions. Its grammar track is unusually heavy for a
beginner course — 11 lessons — and that fact drives this roadmap more than anything else, because
it means a large slice of what most "B1 French" syllabi open with has **already been taught**.

## 2. What A1/A2 already retired

Audited against the beginner tier's 11 grammar lessons. These are **closed** and may only be
recycled, never re-taught as new material:

gender + the three article families + the de-rule · être/avoir/aller/faire · present tense incl. the
irregulars · adjectives, adverbs, comparatives & superlatives · the whole ne…pas family · all three
question forms · possessives, demonstratives, quantities · reflexives + imperative + *conditionnel de
politesse* as fixed forms (`je voudrais`, `pourriez-vous`) · futur proche + futur simple + venir de ·
passé composé with both auxiliaries incl. agreement · imparfait + the PC-vs-imparfait contrast ·
object pronouns + y/en + stressed pronouns · qui/que/où · basic connectors.

This is worth stating plainly because it is where this course diverges from its siblings. The
Spanish intermediate tier had to spend **five** of its 24 lessons on object pronouns, commands,
reflexives, the two simple pasts, and comparatives/relatives. French does not: all five are done.

## 3. What a standard B1–B2 French syllabus still owes the learner

Audited against the CEFR B1/B2 descriptors and the DELF B1/B2 exam specifications.

**A. Mood and the tenses built on it.** Nothing in the beginner tier touches the **subjonctif** —
the single largest gap, and one that CEFR places squarely at B1. The *conditionnel* exists only as
two memorised politeness formulas, not as a tense with forms and uses. And three compound tenses are
missing outright: **plus-que-parfait**, **futur antérieur**, **conditionnel passé** — which in turn
means **si-clauses** can only be taught at type 1.

**B. Four structures with no Spanish counterpart at this tier.** These are what make French B1/B2
bigger than its siblings rather than smaller:

1. **Past-participle agreement with *avoir*** (with a preceding direct object). Spanish has no
   participle agreement at all; French has a rule that interacts with COD pronouns, *que*, reflexives
   and the *faire causatif*, and it is tested in every DELF written paper.
2. **The *faire causatif*.** Spanish paraphrases with *hacer que* + subjunctive. French has a
   dedicated construction with its own pronoun-placement and agreement rules, plus the
   *laisser/voir/entendre* + infinitive family alongside it.
3. **The *dont* / *lequel* / *auquel* / *duquel* paradigm.** Spanish relatives are a short list.
   French's is a table that varies by preposition, gender and number, and *dont* has no English or
   Spanish analogue.
4. **The *-ant* trio** — participe présent, gérondif, adjectif verbal — three forms from one ending,
   distinguished by function and by agreement. Spanish has one invariable gerund.

**C. A literary/journalistic reading register.** French B2 readers meet the **passé simple** (and
behind it the passé antérieur and the subjonctif imparfait) in every novel and much of the press.
Spanish does not need a lesson for this — its preterite is an everyday spoken tense already taught.
French does. *This was not on the original candidate list; it is added here deliberately, because a
B2 who cannot parse `il fut` cannot read authentic text, which is a B2 descriptor.*

**D. A register axis Spanish does not have in the same form.** *Soutenu / courant / familier* is a
real grammatical stratification in French (inversion, *nous* vs *on*, *ne*-dropping, question forms),
and beneath it sits **verlan**, a productive slang-formation system with no Spanish equivalent.

**E. Skills, not just grammar.** DELF B1/B2 tests argumentation, formal correspondence, and
comprehension of authentic media. These need lessons of their own.

**F. The course's own signature.** Every beginner lesson carries a 🇫🇷/🇨🇦 `.dialect-compare` box.
At A1/A2 that comparison is mostly vocabulary and accent. At B2 it becomes register, syntax,
administrative language and media — so Québécois earns a full deep-dive lesson here that the
Spanish and Tagalog siblings have no reason to include.

## 4. Lesson count — the justification

Per [[course-size-sop]], the count is derived, not chosen. The siblings sit at 24 (Spanish
intermediate, Tagalog intermediate); this tier lands at **26**, and neither number was the target.

Working from §3:

| Pressure | Effect on count |
|---|---|
| A1/A2 already closed object pronouns, commands, reflexives, the two pasts, comparatives & basic relatives | **−5** vs the Spanish intermediate syllabus |
| Subjonctif needs three lessons, not one (forms+triggers · conjunctions/impersonals/*ne* explétif · passé + mood choice) — it is the biggest single gap and cramming it produces 4,000-word lessons | **+2** |
| The four no-Spanish-counterpart structures of §3B each need a lesson | **+4** |
| Passé simple / literary reading register (§3C) | **+1** |
| Register ladder **and** argot/verlan are separate skills — recognising formality level is not the same as decoding slang | **+1** |
| Québécois B2 deep dive (§3F) | **+1** |
| Formal writing splits: correspondence vs. CV + lettre de motivation (different genres, different 🇫🇷/🇨🇦 conventions, both DELF-relevant) | **+1** |
| Compound tenses (plus-que-parfait, futur antérieur, conditionnel) that Spanish taught as one "perfect tenses" lesson need three here because each carries its own subordinate-clause syntax | **+1** |

24 − 5 + 11 = **30** on a naive tally; four of those merge honestly — affixes/nominalisation folds
into faux amis as one "decoding vocabulary" lesson, *ne* explétif lives inside the subjunctive
lesson that motivates it, concordance des temps rides with discours indirect (its main arena), and
futur antérieur rides with future-time subordinate clauses. That lands at **26**.

**Why not 24.** Forcing 24 would mean either collapsing the subjunctive into two lessons (the topic
CEFR weights most heavily at B1) or dropping the passé simple and the Québécois deep dive — the
first would break the "one sitting per lesson" rule the beginner tier holds to, and the second two
would cost the course its reading ceiling and its signature. **Why not more.** Everything past 26
was merge-able without loss, and each merge above is a genuine single arc rather than a bundle.

Split: **B1 = 13, B2 = 13.**

## 6. Architecture (mirrors `~/projects/spanish/intermediate`)

- Pages are **numbered**: `/intermediate/french_lesson_N.html`, N = 1…26.
- `/learn.js` and `/glossary.js` at the site root are **already tier-aware** — they detect
  `/intermediate/`, switch to the `french-int-` localStorage namespace and resolve
  `french_lesson_N`. They are **not** copied or forked.
- Per-tier files that DO live here: `vocab-data.js`, `lesson-content.js`, `index.html`,
  `cando.js` + `cando.html`, `cheatsheet.js` + `cheatsheet.html`, `readings.js` + `readings.html`,
  `glossary.html` (loads the shared `/glossary.js`), `build_anki.py`.
- `cando.js` hardcodes its localStorage key — the intermediate copy uses **`french-int-cando`**.
- Companions are built by **splicing a new data block** (CANDO / SHEET / DIALOGUES) into a copy of
  the beginner engine, never by rewriting the engine.
- Footer prev/next in `/intermediate/` is **hand-written**. The root `add_footer_nav.py` governs the
  beginner tier only; its `LESSONS` list is not touched.
- Script tags on an intermediate lesson use absolute paths:
  `/site-nav.js`, `/audio.js`, `/intermediate/vocab-data.js`, `/intermediate/lesson-content.js`, `/learn.js`.

## 7. Conventions (inherited — follow exactly)

- **Never** hand-write "What You'll Learn" or "Key Takeaways" into a page. They come from
  `lesson-content.js` (`objectives` + `summary` + `time` + `level`), or the page renders two.
- Vocabulary tables must head their first two columns **`French`** and **`Pronunciation`** or
  `audio.js` will not attach the 🔊 buttons.
- Exercises: a `.practice-section` wrapping a `ul`/`ol`; each `<li>` carries `_____` (3+ underscores)
  **and** an inline `(Answer: x)`; **one blank per li**; accent-free alternatives after a `|`.
- Reserve `___` for real blanks — use `…` in prose, or QA regexes trip on it.
- Keep the 🇫🇷/🇨🇦 `.dialect-compare` box in every lesson.
- `vocab-data.js` entries are `{fr, pron, en}` with **literal** emoji/accents — never `\u{...}`
  escapes, because `build_anki.py` parses that file as JSON.
- `build_anki.py` here uses its **own** DECK_ID/MODEL_ID, distinct from the beginner deck's.

## 8. Final state (built 2026-09-22)

**26 lessons** — B1 = 1–13, B2 = 14–26, exactly as planned in §5. Nothing was dropped or merged
during the build, so the §4 justification still describes what shipped.

**Data & engine**
- `vocab-data.js` — 520 words across 26 lessons (`{fr, pron, en}`) + `titles` and `levels` maps.
  Deliberately **no `slugs` map**: the pages are numbered, so `learn.js` and `cando.js` fall through
  to `french_lesson_N.html` by themselves.
- `lesson-content.js` — 156 objectives (6 per lesson) and 156 summary points, plus per-lesson time
  and CEFR band. Nothing is hand-written into the pages.
- `/learn.js` and `/glossary.js` were **not touched** — both already detected `/intermediate/`.

**Companions:** `cando.html` (90 descriptors, key `french-int-cando`) · `glossary.html` (520 rows,
shared engine) · `cheatsheet.html` (17 categories, 134 rows — grammar recipes, not survival phrases)
· `readings.html` (8 pieces, 62 lines, 24 questions) · `french_intermediate.apkg`
(603 notes / 1,206 cards).

**Exercises:** every lesson carries exactly 4 `.practice-section` groups — 104 tier-wide, each one
verified to score N/N on reveal-then-check.

**QA (headless Chromium, local `python3 -m http.server`):** see `../../status.md` for the full list.
All 31 intermediate pages and all 33 beginner pages: 0 console errors, 0 broken links (67 targets),
correct prev/next chain and `<h1>` numbering, no answer-key leaks, light + dark + 390px clean, and
localStorage namespaces verified isolated in fresh browser contexts.

**Gotchas found during this build, worth keeping:**
- The lint that guards §7 is worth re-running after any edit: it caught `___` leaking into prose in
  lesson 8 and a two-blanks-in-one-`<li>` exercise in lesson 16, both of which render fine but break
  the exercise engine's one-blank-per-item contract.
- `learn.js`'s `reveal()` fills the **first** alternative before the `|`, so the first alternative
  must always be the fully accented, canonical form.
- An answer may not contain a `)` — the parser regex is `\(answer:\s*([^)]+)\)`.
- `.g-select` in `styles/learn.css` had no `max-width`, so a long lesson-title option in the glossary
  filter pushed the page 125px past a 390px viewport. This was a **pre-existing beginner-tier bug**;
  fixing it in the shared stylesheet fixed both tiers.
