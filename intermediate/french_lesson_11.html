🇫🇷 Lesson 11: The Passive, On, and Se Faire

English loves the passive: the bridge was built, my bag was stolen, it is said that…. French has a passive too, and it is easy to build — but French speakers avoid it far more than English speakers do. Learning the passive is really about learning the three things French uses instead.

🔧 The Passive Itself

être (in whatever tense you need) + past participle, agreeing with the subject.

TenseFrenchMeaning
PrésentLa maison est vendue.The house is sold.
Passé composéLa maison a été vendue.The house was sold.
ImparfaitLa maison était vendue.The house was (being) sold.
FuturLa maison sera vendue.The house will be sold.
ConditionnelLa maison serait vendue.The house would be sold.
Subjonctif…que la maison soit vendue.…that the house be sold.

The participle always agrees with the subject: le pont a été construit, la maison a été construite, les ponts ont été construits.

⚠️ Only direct objects can become subjects

English happily says "I was given a book" or "he was told the truth". French cannot: donner and dire give things to people, so the person is an indirect object and can never become a passive subject. Je suis donné un livre is impossible. French says On m'a donné un livre.

🎭 The Agent: par or de?

If you name who did it, par is the default. De appears with verbs of emotion, and with states rather than actions.

FrenchPronunciationMeaning
Le pont a été construit par Eiffel.luh pohn ah ay-tay kohn-strwee par ay-FELThe bridge was built by Eiffel. (action)
Il est aimé de tous.eel ay tay-may duh TOOSHe is loved by everyone. (feeling)
Elle est respectée de ses collègues.el ay res-pek-tay duh say ko-LEGShe is respected by her colleagues.
La table était couverte de poussière.lah tahbl ay-tay koo-vairt duh poo-SYAIRThe table was covered in dust. (state)
La route est bordée d'arbres.lah root ay bor-day DARBRThe road is lined with trees.

Most of the time, though, French simply leaves the agent out — and then reaches for one of the alternatives below instead.

1️⃣ Alternative: On

This is the big one. Where English reaches for a passive to avoid naming a doer, French reaches for on. It is shorter, more natural, and overwhelmingly more common in speech.

English passiveNatural FrenchStiff French passive
My bag was stolen.On m'a volé mon sac.Mon sac a été volé.
I was told that…On m'a dit que…— (impossible)
It is said that…On dit que…Il est dit que… (rare)
French is spoken here.On parle français ici.Le français est parlé ici.
We were asked to wait.On nous a demandé d'attendre.— (impossible)

Note the fourth row: on even rescues sentences where the French passive is technically legal but sounds like a translation. If in doubt, use on.

2️⃣ Alternative: the Pronominal Passive

For general truths, habits and instructions, French uses a reflexive form with a thing as the subject. There is no doer at all, stated or implied.

FrenchPronunciationMeaning
Ça se dit.sah suh DEEPeople say that. / That's said.
Ça ne se fait pas.sah nuh suh fay PAHThat isn't done. (social rule)
Ce plat se mange froid.suh plah suh mahnzh FRWAHThis dish is eaten cold.
Le vin blanc se sert frais.luh van blahn suh sair FRAYWhite wine is served chilled.
Ça s'écrit comment ?sah say-kree ko-MAHNHow is that written?
Les billets se vendent en ligne.lay bee-yay suh vahnd ahn LEE-nyuhTickets are sold online.

3️⃣ Alternative: Se faire + Infinitive

When something happens to the subject — whether they arranged it or suffered it — French uses se faire + infinitive. This is extremely common and has no clean English equivalent beyond "get".

FrenchPronunciationMeaning
se faire volersuh fair vo-LAYto get robbed
se faire renversersuh fair rahn-vair-SAYto get knocked down
se faire couper les cheveuxsuh fair koo-pay lay shuh-VUHto get one's hair cut
se faire opérersuh fair o-pay-RAYto have an operation
Il s'est fait avoir.eel say fay tah-VWAHRHe got taken in / conned.
Je me suis fait voler mon vélo.zhuh muh swee fay vo-lay mohn vay-LOHI got my bike stolen.

There is a close cousin, se voir + infinitive, used in more formal registers: Il s'est vu refuser l'entrée — "he was refused entry". You will meet faire + infinitive properly in Lesson 14; note for now that fait in these constructions is invariable: elle s'est fait voler, never faite.

🇫🇷 France

The true passive belongs to administrative and journalistic French: Le décret a été publié au Journal officiel. In conversation it can sound pompous — French speakers will almost always choose on. Note too the formal set phrases être chargé de ("to be responsible for") and il est interdit de, which are passives in form but function as fixed expressions.

🇨🇦 Canada

Same structures. Québécois is, if anything, even more attached to on and to se faire: J'me suis fait passer un sapin ("I got swindled" — literally "had a fir tree passed to me") is a wonderfully Québécois use of the construction. Watch also for calques from English in casual speech, such as Il a été donné un prix, which is non-standard in both varieties.

✍️ Practice

Exercise 1 — Build the passive

Fill in être in the right tense, agreeing with the subject.

  1. La maison _____ vendue l'année dernière. (passé composé: a …) (Answer: a été|a ete)
  2. Les documents _____ signés demain. (futur) (Answer: seront)
  3. Cette église _____ construite au XIIe siècle. (passé composé) (Answer: a été|a ete)
  4. Le film _____ apprécié de tous. (présent) (Answer: est)

Exercise 2 — par or de?

  • Le pont a été construit _____ Eiffel. (Answer: par)
  • Il est aimé _____ tous. (Answer: de)
  • La lettre a été écrite _____ ma sœur. (Answer: par)
  • La table était couverte _____ poussière. (Answer: de)

Exercise 3 — Rewrite with on

Fill in the missing word so the sentence uses on.

  1. My bag was stolen. → _____ m'a volé mon sac. (Answer: On|on)
  2. I was told to wait. → On m'a _____ d'attendre. (Answer: dit)
  3. French is spoken here. → _____ parle français ici. (Answer: On|on)
  4. The flight was cancelled. → On a _____ le vol. (Answer: annulé|annule)

Exercise 4 — Se faire

  • He got robbed. → Il s'est fait _____. (Answer: voler)
  • She had her hair cut. → Elle s'est fait _____ les cheveux. (Answer: couper)
  • I got knocked down by a bike. → Je me suis fait _____ par un vélo. (Answer: renverser)
