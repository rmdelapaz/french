💡 "J'ai mal à..." — The Key Structure

To say something hurts, use J'ai mal à + definite article + body part:
J'ai mal à la tête — I have a headache
J'ai mal au ventre — My stomach hurts (à + le = au)
J'ai mal aux dents — My teeth hurt (à + les = aux)
J'ai mal à l'épaule — My shoulder hurts

🇫🇷 Healthcare in France

France has a universal healthcare system. You visit a médecin généraliste (GP) and pay upfront (around €25 for a consultation), then get reimbursed by la Sécurité sociale (social security). Pharmacies (pharmacies) are marked with a green cross and pharmacists can advise on minor ailments.

🇨🇦 Healthcare in Canada

Québec has publicly funded healthcare through la RAMQ (Régie de l'assurance maladie du Québec). Doctor visits are free with a health card (la carte d'assurance maladie / carte soleil). Emergency rooms: l'urgence. Walk-in clinics: une clinique sans rendez-vous. Wait times can be long — "Prenez un numéro."

⚠️ Pharmacy Vocabulary Difference

Painkiller names differ: France uses paracétamol (brand: Doliprane), while Canada uses acétaminophène (brand: Tylenol). Same drug — different name! Ibuprofène is the same in both.

📚 Key Takeaways

📌 Lesson Summary

✦ Use "J'ai mal à + article + body part" to express pain: J'ai mal à la tête, au dos, aux dents.

✦ "Depuis" + time = "since/for" (how long you've had symptoms).

✦ Emergency: 112 in France (+ 15/17/18), 911 in Canada.

✦ Paracétamol (🇫🇷) = acétaminophène (🇨🇦) = Tylenol.

✦ France: pay-and-reimburse system. Canada: free with provincial health card.
