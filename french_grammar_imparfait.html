🇫🇷 Lesson 21: The Imparfait & Choosing Your Past Tense

🎬 Two Pasts, Two Jobs

You already have the passé composé for events. The imparfait does something different: it paints the background. If the passé composé is the action on screen, the imparfait is the set, the weather, the mood and the music playing behind it.

💡 The Film Analogy

Il pleuvait et je marchais dans la rue — camera pans across a rainy street, someone walking. That's the imparfait: no beginning, no end, just a scene in progress.
Soudain, une voiture s'est arrêtée — something happens. That's the passé composé: one completed event that moves the story forward.

🔧 Building the Imparfait — One Rule, One Exception

Take the nous form of the present tense, remove -ons, and add the endings. That's it — for every verb in the language except être.

✅ Four Forms Sound the Same Again

parlais, parlais, parlait, parlaient are all "par-LAY". Only nous (-ions) and vous (-iez) break the pattern — exactly like the present tense. The good news: one set of endings covers every verb in French.

📋 What the Imparfait Is For

JobExampleEnglish
Ongoing action in the pastJe regardais la télé.I was watching TV.
Habit or repeated actionChaque été, on allait en Bretagne.Every summer we used to go to Brittany.

🚨 The "Interrupted Action" Pattern

Whenever one past action interrupts another, French splits the tenses cleanly:
imparfait (what was going on) + quand + passé composé (what cut in).
Je dormais quand le téléphone a sonné. (I was sleeping when the phone rang.)
Get this one pattern automatic and most of your past-tense choices fall out of it.

Same Verb, Different Tense, Different Meaning

VerbImparfaitPassé composé
savoirje savais = I knewj'ai su = I found out
vouloirje voulais = I wantedj'ai voulu = I tried / I decided to
pouvoirje pouvais = I was able toj'ai pu = I managed to

💡 The Pattern Behind the Pairs

The imparfait describes a state that was simply there; the passé composé marks the moment it began. Je savais = the knowledge was in my head. J'ai su = the instant I learned it. Once you see that, you never have to memorise these pairs separately.

📖 Telling a Story

Real narration alternates constantly between the two. Read this aloud and notice how the imparfait sets each scene and the passé composé keeps the plot moving:

C'était un samedi matin. Il faisait beau et je n'avais rien à faire. Je suis allé au marché, j'ai acheté du pain et du fromage, et je suis rentré. Pendant que je préparais le déjeuner, mon voisin a frappé à la porte. Il voulait du sel. Nous avons parlé pendant une heure.

It was a Saturday morning. The weather was nice and I had nothing to do. I went to the market, bought some bread and cheese, and came home. While I was making lunch, my neighbour knocked at the door. He wanted some salt. We talked for an hour.

⚠️ pendant que vs quand

Pendant que (while) introduces two things going on at once — both usually imparfait: Pendant qu'il lisait, elle cuisinait.
Quand (when) usually introduces the interruption: Il lisait quand elle est arrivée.

🇫🇷🇨🇦 Across the Atlantic

🇫🇷 France

Si on allait...? is the standard way to float a plan, and the imparfait softens requests in shops: Je voulais juste demander... ("I just wanted to ask...") — it sounds gentler than the present.

🇨🇦 Canada

Same tense, same rules. In Québécois storytelling you'll hear j'étais reduced to "j'tais" and il y avait to "y'avait". Nostalgic narration — Dans mon temps, on jouait dehors tout l'hiver — is a favourite register.
