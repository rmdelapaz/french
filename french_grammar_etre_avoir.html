🇫🇷 Lesson 5: Être, Avoir & the Essential Verbs

👤 First, the Subject Pronouns

Before any verb can be conjugated you need the six people it can belong to. French has one extra wrinkle English doesn't: two words for "you", and a pronoun (on) that quietly does the work of "we" in everyday speech.

💡 On Is How People Actually Say "We"

Textbooks teach nous allons; real conversations say on va. On always takes the il/elle verb form, which makes it the easiest pronoun in the language: On mange ? (Shall we eat?), On y va ! (Let's go!), On parle français ici. (We / people speak French here.)

🟦 Être — To Be

Être is the most irregular verb in French and the most useful. It identifies, describes and locates.

⚠️ No Article With Jobs and Nationalities

English says "She is a teacher." French drops the article after être: Elle est professeure. Say Elle est une professeure and you'll be understood, but you'll sound translated. The article comes back only with c'est: C'est une professeure formidable.

Avoir Expressions: States French "Has" Instead of "Is"

This is one of the sharpest differences between the two languages. Where English says "I am hungry", French says "I have hunger". Learn these as whole blocks.

FrenchPronunciationMeaning
J'ai faim.zhay FANI'm hungry.
J'ai soif.zhay SWAHFI'm thirsty.
J'ai chaud / froid.zhay SHOH / FRWAHI'm hot / cold.
J'ai vingt-cinq ans.zhay vant-SANK ahnI'm twenty-five years old.
J'ai peur.zhay PUHRI'm afraid.
J'ai sommeil.zhay so-MAYI'm sleepy.
J'ai raison / tort.zhay ray-ZOHN / TORI'm right / wrong.
J'ai besoin de...zhay buh-ZWAN duhI need...
J'ai envie de...zhay ahn-VEE duhI feel like... / I want...

🚨 Never "Je suis 25 ans"

Saying Je suis vingt-cinq ans means something close to "I am twenty-five years" — it marks you instantly as a beginner. Age is always avoir: J'ai vingt-cinq ans. And the word ans can never be dropped the way English drops "years old".

Aller also carries the everyday question Comment ça va ? and, followed by an infinitive, expresses the near future — Je vais manger (I'm going to eat). You'll meet that fully in the future-tense lesson.

Faire covers far more ground than "to do": weather (il fait beau), sports (faire du vélo), chores (faire la vaisselle), shopping (faire les courses) and cooking (faire la cuisine).

🔍 C'est vs Il est — The Classic Confusion

UsePatternExample
C'est + article + nounidentifying somethingC'est un bon restaurant.
C'est + nameidentifying a personC'est Marie.
C'est + adjective (general)reacting to a whole situationC'est difficile !
Il / Elle est + adjectivedescribing a specific person or thingElle est intelligente.
Il / Elle est + job / nationalityno articleIl est ingénieur.

✅ A Test That Works

If the next word is an article (un, une, le, la, des), you need c'est. If the next word is a bare adjective or job title describing one identified person, you need il / elle est. C'est un médecin and Il est médecin are both correct — they just enter the sentence differently.

📍 Il y a — There Is / There Are

One fixed phrase covers both singular and plural, and it is your fastest tool for describing any place.

FrenchPronunciationMeaning

Il n'y a pas de place.eel nee ah pah duh PLAHSThere's no room.

🇫🇷🇨🇦 Across the Atlantic

🇫🇷 France

Il y a collapses to "ya" in speech: Y a un problème. Greeting answers stay compact — Ça va, Ça roule, Nickel. Vous êtes is expected with any stranger, including shop staff and bus drivers.

🇨🇦 Canada

Québec says y'a just as often, and adds the questioning particle -tu: T'es-tu prêt ? (Are you ready?). Québécois switches to tu far sooner than France does — shop staff may tutoyer you straight away, and it is friendly rather than rude.

✍️ Practice

Exercise 1 — Être or avoir?

Write the correct form of être or avoir.

  1. Je _____ américain. (I am American) (Answer: suis)
  2. Elle _____ trente ans. (She is thirty) (Answer: a)
  3. Nous _____ en retard. (We are late) (Answer: sommes)
  4. Vous _____ soif ? (Are you thirsty?) (Answer: avez)
  5. Ils _____ étudiants. (They are students) (Answer: sont)
  6. Tu _____ raison. (You're right) (Answer: as)

Exercise 2 — C'est or il/elle est?

  1. _____ un très bon film. (Answer: C'est|Cest)
  2. _____ ingénieure dans une grande entreprise. (she) (Answer: Elle est)
  3. _____ difficile, le subjonctif ! (Answer: C'est|Cest)
  4. _____ ma soeur. (Answer: C'est|Cest)

Exercise 3 — Aller and faire

  1. Nous _____ au marché le samedi. (aller) (Answer: allons)
  2. Qu'est-ce que tu _____ ce soir ? (faire) (Answer: fais)
  3. Il _____ beau aujourd'hui. (faire — weather) (Answer: fait)
  4. Je _____ à Québec en juillet. (aller) (Answer: vais)
