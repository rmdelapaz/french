🇫🇷 Lesson 12: Possessives, Demonstratives & Quantities

🔑 Possessives Agree With the Thing, Not the Owner

This is the single most counter-intuitive rule in French for English speakers. In English, "his" and "her" tell you about the owner. In French, son and sa tell you about the object.

Son frère can mean "his brother" or "her brother" — because frère is masculine. Sa soeur can mean "his sister" or "her sister" — because soeur is feminine. The owner's gender simply isn't encoded.

⚠️ mon amie, Not ma amie

Before a feminine noun that starts with a vowel sound, the masculine form is used — purely for pronunciation: mon amie, ton école, son histoire. The noun is still feminine, so any adjective still agrees: mon amie est gentille.

Saying Whose: the de Construction

French has no apostrophe-s. Ownership is expressed backwards, with de:

FrenchPronunciationMeaning
le livre de Marieluh leevr duh mah-REEMarie's book
la voiture de mon frèrelah vwah-tür duh mohn FRAIRmy brother's car
C'est à moi.say tah MWAHIt's mine.
C'est à qui ?say tah KEEWhose is this?

💡 de + le = du, de + les = des

These contractions are compulsory: la voiture du professeur (not de le), les livres des étudiants (not de les). De la and de l' stay as they are. The same merging happens with à: à + le = au, à + les = aux — je vais au cinéma, aux États-Unis.

👉 Demonstratives: This, That, These, Those

French makes no basic distinction between "this" and "that". One set of words covers both.

FormUsed withExample
cemasculine singularce livre — this / that book
cetmasculine singular before a vowel soundcet homme, cet appartement
cettefeminine singularcette femme, cette idée
cesall pluralsces livres, ces maisons

When you really must distinguish, add -ci (here) or -là (there) to the noun: ce livre-ci (this book here) vs ce livre-là (that book there). In practice, -là does most of the work: Je prends celui-là (I'll take that one).

🚨 The Exception: bien des and la plupart des

Two expressions keep the full article: bien des gens (a good many people) and la plupart des étudiants (most students). Everything else in this lesson takes bare de.

💡 Note the Difference: quelques vs un peu de

Quelques counts separate items: quelques amis, quelques minutes. Un peu de measures a mass: un peu de sucre, un peu de temps. English "a few" vs "a little" splits exactly the same way.

🇫🇷🇨🇦 Across the Atlantic

🇫🇷 France

Shopping happens in metric quantities you'll hear constantly: une plaquette de beurre, une botte de radis, 500 grammes de ("cinq cents grammes de"). At the market, Et avec ceci ? ("Anything else?") is the standard prompt.

🇨🇦 Canada

Québec mixes metric and imperial: you buy une pinte de lait (a quart) and une livre de beurre (a pound) alongside kilos. Une caisse de 24 is a case of beer — famously called une caisse de vingt-quatre or just une 24.

✍️ Practice

Exercise 1 — Possessives

  1. C'est _____ frère. (my — frère is masculine) (Answer: mon)
  2. Voici _____ soeur. (my — soeur is feminine) (Answer: ma)
  3. Ce sont _____ parents. (my — plural) (Answer: mes)
  4. _____ amie s'appelle Claire. (my — feminine noun, vowel start!) (Answer: Mon)
  5. Ils adorent _____ enfants. (their — plural) (Answer: leurs)
  6. C'est la voiture _____ mon frère. (possession) (Answer: de)

Exercise 2 — Demonstratives

  1. _____ livre est intéressant. (this — masculine) (Answer: Ce)
  2. _____ femme est ma voisine. (this — feminine) (Answer: Cette)
  3. _____ homme travaille ici. (this — masculine, vowel start) (Answer: Cet)
  4. _____ pommes sont chères. (these) (Answer: Ces)

Exercise 3 — Quantities

  1. J'ai beaucoup _____ travail. (Answer: de)
  2. Je voudrais une bouteille _____ vin rouge. (Answer: de)
  3. Il y a _____ de sel dans la soupe ! (too much) (Answer: trop)
  4. Je prends un peu _____ sucre. (Answer: de)
  5. Je vais à la piscine tous _____ jours. (every day) (Answer: les)
