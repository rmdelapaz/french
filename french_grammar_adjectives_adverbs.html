🇫🇷 Lesson 9: Adjectives, Adverbs & Comparisons

🎨 Adjectives Agree — Always

An English adjective never changes: a green book, a green house, green books. A French adjective is a chameleon: it takes the gender and number of the noun it describes, every single time.

MasculineFeminineMasculine pluralFeminine plural
vertvertevertsvertes
petitpetitepetitspetites
françaisfrançaisefrançaisfrançaises
contentcontentecontentscontentes

The default recipe: add -e for feminine, -s for plural, -es for both. If the masculine already ends in -e (rouge, jeune, facile), the feminine is identical. If it already ends in -s or -x (français, heureux), the masculine plural doesn't change.

✅ The Feminine -e Is What You Hear

That silent-looking final -e wakes up the consonant before it. Petit is "puh-TEE" but petite is "puh-TEET". Grand is "grahn", grande is "grahnd". So agreement isn't just spelling — it is audible, and getting it wrong is noticeable.

💡 Why bel, nouvel, vieil Exist

French avoids a clash of vowel sounds. Before a masculine noun starting with a vowel, beau borrows a softer form: un bel appartement, un nouvel ami, un vieil homme. It's the same instinct as l' instead of le.

📍 Where the Adjective Goes

The default in French is after the noun — the opposite of English. Une voiture rouge, un film intéressant, un repas délicieux.

But a small, very common set goes before. Remember them with BANGS: Beauty, Age, Number, Goodness, Size.

CategoryAdjectivesExample
Beautybeau, joliune jolie maison
Agejeune, vieux, nouveauun jeune homme
Numberpremier, deuxième, dernierle premier jour
Goodnessbon, mauvais, meilleurun bon restaurant
Sizegrand, petit, gros, longune grande ville

⚠️ Some Adjectives Change Meaning With Position

Un ancien professeur = a former teacher · un professeur ancien = an ancient teacher.
Un grand homme = a great man · un homme grand = a tall man.
Ma propre chambre = my own room · ma chambre propre = my clean room.
Before the noun tends to be figurative; after the noun tends to be literal.

🏃 Adverbs — Describing the Verb

To build most adverbs, take the feminine adjective and add -ment. It is the direct equivalent of English "-ly".

🚨 Bon vs Bien — The Mistake Everyone Makes

Bon is an adjective (describes a noun): un bon film, une bonne idée.
Bien is an adverb (describes a verb): Il parle bien, Ça va bien.
"The food is good" = C'est bon. "You did well" = C'est bien. Same trap with mauvais (adjective) and mal (adverb).

Where Adverbs Sit

Short adverbs go straight after the conjugated verb, not between the subject and the verb as in English.

⚖️ Making Comparisons

Three little words do all the work: plus (more), moins (less), aussi (as). The thing you compare to follows que.

FrenchPronunciationMeaning
Paris est plus grand que Lyon.pah-REE ay plü grahn kuh lee-OHNParis is bigger than Lyon.
Le métro est moins cher que le taxi.luh may-TROH ay mwan SHAIRThe metro is less expensive than the taxi.
Elle est aussi rapide que moi.el ay oh-SEE rah-PEED kuh mwahShe's as fast as me.
Je travaille plus que toi.zhuh trah-VYE plü kuh twahI work more than you.

Superlatives: the Most, the Least

Add the definite article to the comparative: le / la / les plus or le / la / les moins. If the adjective normally follows the noun, the superlative does too — and the article repeats.

FrenchPronunciationMeaning
C'est le plus grand musée de Paris.say luh plü grahn mü-ZAYIt's the biggest museum in Paris.
C'est le restaurant le plus cher de la ville.say luh res-toh-RAHN luh plü SHAIRIt's the most expensive restaurant in town.
C'est la moins chère des options.say lah mwan SHAIRIt's the cheapest of the options.

⚠️ "In" After a Superlative Is de, Not dans

"The best restaurant in Montréal" = le meilleur restaurant de Montréal. Using dans here is a classic English-speaker slip.

Ce vin est meilleur. (This wine is better — adjective.) Elle chante mieux. (She sings better — adverb.)

🇫🇷🇨🇦 Across the Atlantic

🇫🇷 France

Intensifiers move fast in France: trop bien, super sympa, hyper cher, grave bon among younger speakers. Vachement ("really, seriously") is informal but universal: C'est vachement bien.

🇨🇦 Canada

Québec's all-purpose intensifier is ben (from bien): C'est ben bon ! You'll also hear pas pire ("not bad", i.e. pretty good) as a compliment and le fun as an adjective: C'était le fun ! (That was fun!)

✍️ Practice

Exercise 1 — Make the adjective agree

  1. une maison _____ (blanc) (Answer: blanche)
  2. des voitures _____ (vert) (Answer: vertes)
  3. une femme _____ (heureux) (Answer: heureuse)
  4. un _____ appartement (beau — before a vowel!) (Answer: bel)
  5. des amis _____ (gentil) (Answer: gentils)

Exercise 2 — Adjective or adverb?

  1. Ce gâteau est très _____. (bon / bien) (Answer: bon)
  2. Il parle _____ anglais. (bon / bien) (Answer: bien)
  3. Elle conduit _____. (lent → adverb) (Answer: lentement)
  4. C'est une _____ idée. (bon) (Answer: bonne)

Exercise 3 — Compare

  1. Le train est _____ rapide que le bus. (more) (Answer: plus)
  2. Ce restaurant est _____ cher que l'autre. (less) (Answer: moins)
  3. Elle est _____ grande que moi. (as) (Answer: aussi)
  4. C'est le _____ restaurant de la ville. (best — irregular!) (Answer: meilleur)
  5. C'est le plus beau musée _____ Paris. (in) (Answer: de)
